## Venture capital and accelerators

VC firms back young companies for a stake. Modern VC dates to 1946 and Georges Doriot. In 2026, AI rounds for OpenAI and Anthropic pushed venture funding to records, and Sequoia's India arm now runs as **Peak XV**.

| Firm | Founded | Founder / leader | Name and story | Famous bets and trivia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ARDC** (American Research and Development Corporation) | 1946, Boston | **Georges Doriot** | Called the "father of venture capitalism". A Harvard professor, he also founded the business school **INSEAD**. | Put $70,000 into **Digital Equipment Corporation** (1957); the stake was later worth about $355 m, often called the first VC home run. |
| **Sequoia Capital** | 1972, Menlo Park | Don Valentine; now led by Alfred Lin and Pat Grady | Named after the giant **redwood trees**, built to last. | Apple, Google, YouTube, WhatsApp, Nvidia, Airbnb. In 2024 it split into three: Sequoia (US/Europe), **Peak XV** (India and South-East Asia) and **HongShan** (China). |
| **Kleiner Perkins** | 1972, Menlo Park | Eugene Kleiner (one of the "traitorous eight" who left Shockley to found Fairchild) and Tom Perkins | — | Amazon, Google, Genentech. Its office is on Sand Hill Road, Silicon Valley's VC street. |
| **Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)** | 2009 | Marc Andreessen (Netscape) and Ben Horowitz | **a16z** is a numeronym: "a", 16 letters, "z". | Andreessen's essay "Software is eating the world" (2011). Raised about $15 bn in early 2026. Horowitz wrote *The Hard Thing About Hard Things*. |
| **Benchmark** | 1995 | Bill Gurley and others | Every partner gets an equal share of profits. | eBay, Uber, Twitter, Snap. |
| **Accel** | 1983 | Arthur Patterson and Jim Swartz | — | Facebook (2005). In India it was **Flipkart**'s first institutional investor. |
| **Founders Fund** | 2005 | Peter Thiel | — | Thiel made the first outside investment in Facebook ($500,000, 2004), and later backed SpaceX and Palantir. |
| **Y Combinator** | 2005 | Paul Graham, Jessica Livingston and others | Named after the **Y combinator**, a function in lambda calculus. | The first famous start-up accelerator: Airbnb, Stripe, Dropbox, Reddit, Coinbase. It also runs the site **Hacker News**. |
| **SoftBank Vision Fund** | 2017 | **Masayoshi Son** | About $100 bn, backed by Saudi Arabia's PIF. | WeWork, Uber, OYO, Paytm, and a big OpenAI investor. Son was an early Alibaba backer and has a "300-year vision". |
| **Tiger Global** | 2001 | Chase Coleman | A "Tiger cub" of Julian Robertson's Tiger Management. | Flipkart's big early cheque. Its 2021 spree was the peak of the start-up bubble. |
| **Lightspeed / General Catalyst / Greylock / Index** | — | — | — | Lightspeed backed Snap, and in India Byju's and OYO. Greylock is Reid Hoffman's firm. |

**Key terms:** limited partners (LPs) give money to general partners (GPs), who usually charge "2 and 20": a 2% annual fee plus 20% of profits ("carry"). Funds are usually ten years long. See the market-terms doc for unicorns and down rounds.

## Private equity and sovereign wealth funds

Private equity buys mature companies, often with borrowed money, and sells them years later. The best name story: **Blackstone** translates its founders' surnames (*Schwarz* = black, *Peter* = stone). Many of these firms have 2026 Kerala links: Blackstone in Federal Bank, Bain in Manappuram, Brookfield in the Leela.

## Hedge funds and trading firms

Hedge funds pool rich investors' money and can bet both ways, including by shorting. The first was run by a journalist. The most successful is run by mathematicians. The most famous collapse, LTCM, had two Nobel laureates. The biggest Indian story is SEBI's 2025 ban on Jane Street.

| Firm | Founded | Founder / leader | Story | Trivia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A.W. Jones & Co.** | 1949 | **Alfred Winslow Jones** | The **first hedge fund**: he bought some stocks and shorted others to "hedge" against the market. | Jones was a sociologist and *Fortune* journalist. He also invented the 20% performance fee. |
| **Bridgewater Associates** | 1975, Connecticut | **Ray Dalio** (started from his two-bedroom flat); CEO Nir Bar Dea | The world's largest hedge fund. | "Radical transparency": meetings are recorded and colleagues rate each other. Dalio's book is *Principles*. |
| **Renaissance Technologies** | 1982 | **Jim Simons**, a mathematician and former code-breaker (died 2024) | Pure maths and data, no economists. | Its **Medallion Fund** made about 66% a year before fees from 1988, and is closed to outsiders. Simons won geometry's Oswald Veblen Prize, for the Chern–Simons theory. |
| **Citadel** | 1990, Chicago (now Miami) | **Ken Griffin** | Griffin started trading **from his Harvard dorm** in 1987, with a satellite dish on the roof. | Its sister firm Citadel Securities handles a huge share of US retail trades. Griffin paid $43 m for a copy of the US Constitution. |
| **D.E. Shaw** | 1988, New York | David E. Shaw, a computer scientist | An early quant fund. | **Jeff Bezos** was a vice-president there and left in 1994 to found Amazon. |
| **Two Sigma / Millennium / Point72** | — | John Overdeck and David Siegel / Izzy Englander / **Steve Cohen** | Point72 came out of **SAC Capital**, which pleaded guilty to insider trading (2013). | Cohen inspired Bobby Axelrod in *Billions* and owns the New York Mets. |
| **Man Group** | 1783, London | — | Began as a sugar and rum broker, supplying the Royal Navy's rum ration. | Sponsored the **Man Booker Prize** (2002–19). |
| **Soros Fund Management (Quantum Fund)** | 1970 | **George Soros** | Black Wednesday (1992): he shorted the pound and made about $1 bn. | Called "the man who broke the Bank of England". |
| **Tiger Management** | 1980 | **Julian Robertson** | Its many proteges are called the "**Tiger cubs**". | Cubs include Tiger Global, Viking and Lone Pine. |
| **Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM)** | 1994 | John Meriwether, with **Myron Scholes and Robert Merton** (Nobel 1997) | Collapsed in 1998 and was rescued by a Fed-organised group of banks. | Book: *When Genius Failed*. |
| **Elliott Management** | 1977 | **Paul Singer** | A famously aggressive activist investor. | In 2012 it had an Argentine navy training ship, the *ARA Libertad*, seized in Ghana over unpaid debts. |
| **Pershing Square** | 2004 | **Bill Ackman** | Activist. | Lost about $1 bn on its campaign against Herbalife. |
| **Scion Capital** | 2000 | **Michael Burry** | Bet against US mortgages before 2008. | Christian Bale played him in *The Big Short*. |
| **Melvin Capital / Archegos** | — | Gabe Plotkin / Bill Hwang | Melvin was crushed by the GameStop squeeze (2021). Archegos, Hwang's family office, collapsed in 2021 and cost banks over $10 bn. | Hwang was a "Tiger cub". |
| **Jane Street** | 2000, New York | Trading firm | A quantitative trading firm famous for its puzzles. Sam Bankman-Fried worked there. | In **July 2025 SEBI barred it from Indian markets** and impounded about **₹4,843 crore**, alleging it manipulated Bank Nifty options expiries. Its appeal at the Securities Appellate Tribunal was still running in 2026. |
| **Hindenburg Research** | 2017 | Nate Anderson | A short seller, named after the 1937 airship disaster as a man-made, avoidable crash. | Its Adani report (Jan 2023) knocked over $100 bn off the group's value. It shut down in Jan 2025. |

## Asset managers, banks, cards and rating agencies

The neatest connect: **BlackRock** began inside **Blackstone**, and kept the "Black". Also new in 2026: **Greg Abel** became CEO of Berkshire Hathaway on 1 Jan, with Warren Buffett staying on as chairman.

### Asset managers and holding companies

| Firm | Founded | Leader | Story | Trivia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **BlackRock** | 1988 | **Larry Fink** | Started as Blackstone Financial Management, a bond unit inside Blackstone, and spun off in 1992. | The world's largest asset manager (over $11 tn). Its **Aladdin** risk software runs much of Wall Street. It bought **iShares** ETFs from Barclays in 2009. |
| **Vanguard** | 1975 | Founded by **Jack Bogle** | Bogle named it after **HMS Vanguard**, Nelson's flagship at the Battle of the Nile (1798). Its funds' investors own the company. | Launched the first index fund for ordinary investors (1976), mocked as "Bogle's Folly". |
| **State Street** | 1792, Boston | — | One of the oldest US banks. | Launched **SPDR (SPY)**, the first US ETF (1993). It commissioned *Fearless Girl*. |
| **Fidelity** | 1946, Boston | Johnson family | — | Peter Lynch ran its **Magellan Fund** (1977–90). |
| **Berkshire Hathaway** | Textile mill; Buffett took control in 1965 | **Greg Abel** (CEO from 1 Jan 2026); Warren Buffett, chairman | Buffett bought it partly out of spite after its boss tried to cut the price of a share buyback. He later called it his worst investment. | Its annual meeting in Omaha is called "Woodstock for Capitalists". A Class A share is the world's most expensive stock. Charlie Munger died in Nov 2023. |

## Indian and Kerala financial groups

Kerala is India's gold-loan and chit-fund capital, and Thrissur is at its centre: KSFE, three old private banks, Manappuram and ESAF are all based there. Nationally, the stories to know are LIC's IPO, the HDFC merger and Zerodha's name.

| Group | Founded | Founder / leader | Story | Trivia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LIC** | 1956 | State-owned | Formed by nationalising about 245 insurance companies. | Its motto is *Yogakshemam vahamyaham*, from the Bhagavad Gita. Its 2022 IPO (₹21,008 crore) was India's largest until Hyundai's in 2024. It is the stock market's biggest domestic investor. |
| **SBI** | 1806 (Bank of Calcutta) | Government-owned | Bank of Calcutta → Imperial Bank of India (1921) → State Bank of India (1955). | India's largest bank. SBI Mutual Fund is the largest fund house. |

| **ICICI Bank** | 1955 (ICICI) | K.V. Kamath built the modern bank | Set up with World Bank backing to fund industry. | — |
| **Kotak Mahindra Bank** | 1985 | **Uday Kotak** | Started with about ₹10 lakh. **Anand Mahindra** was an early investor, hence the name. | The first NBFC to convert into a bank (2003). |

| **Paytm (One97)** | 2010 | Vijay Shekhar Sharma | Paytm = "Pay Through Mobile". | The RBI shut down Paytm Payments Bank's main business in 2024. |
| **Rare Enterprises** | — | **Rakesh Jhunjhunwala** (died 2022), the "Big Bull" | **Ra**kesh + **Re**kha (his wife). | Titan was his famous bet. He co-founded Akasa Air. |
