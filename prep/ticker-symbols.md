**The rule of thumb:** NYSE tickers used to be 1–3 letters and Nasdaq 4–5, but since 2007 companies can move freely between exchanges and keep their tickers.\*

### Share classes and odd suffixes

- **BRK.A / BRK.B**: Berkshire Hathaway. Class A **has never split** and is the world's priciest share (above $700,000). Buffett created BRK.B in 1996 to stop unit trusts from repackaging A shares.
- **GOOGL / GOOG**: Alphabet's **Class A (voting)** and **Class C (no vote)**. Class B (10 votes each) is held by the founders. The 2014 stock split created the non-voting GOOG class; Alphabet itself was formed in 2015.
- **META**: Facebook changed **FB → META** on 9 Jun 2022. It took the ticker from the **Roundhill Metaverse ETF**, which became METV.\*
- **TWTR**: Twitter was delisted after Musk's takeover (Oct 2022). At its 2013 IPO, investors bought **TWTRQ** (bankrupt **Tweeter Home Entertainment**) by mistake, and it jumped about 1,000%.\*

### Mistaken-identity rallies (favourite quiz material)

- **ZOOM vs ZM**: in 2019 and again in Mar 2020, investors bought tiny Chinese **Zoom Technologies** (ZOOM) instead of Zoom Video (ZM). The SEC suspended trading, and ZOOM became ZTNO.\*
- **SIGL**: after Musk tweeted "**Use Signal**" (Jan 2021), **Signal Advance** jumped more than 5,000% even though it had nothing to do with the app.\*
- **Clubhouse Media (CMGR)**: rallied on confusion with the Clubhouse app (2021).\*
- **Pattern**: a mistaken-ticker rally almost always follows a hot IPO or a celebrity tweet.

### The word "ticker"

- The **stock ticker** was invented by **Edward Calahan** (1867), and **Thomas Edison** improved it (the Universal Stock Printer). It's named after the **ticking** sound.
- **Ticker-tape parades** in New York's "Canyon of Heroes" began with the Statue of Liberty dedication in 1886, when office workers threw used tape.\*
