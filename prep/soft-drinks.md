The companion to the alcohol-brands page: fizzy drinks, their patent-medicine beginnings and the famous flops. Several of these are genuine convergent-deduction chains rather than recall questions.

## Quiz-ready stories

1. **Fanta** was invented in **Nazi Germany** in 1941, when the US embargo cut Coca-Cola's German plant off from Coke syrup. The name is short for *Fantasie*, "imagination".
2. **7 Up** was launched as "Bib-Label Lithiated Lemon-Lime Soda" and genuinely contained **lithium citrate**, a mood stabiliser, until **1948**.
3. **Pepsi** is named for **dyspepsia** — pharmacist Caleb Bradham sold it as a digestive remedy.
4. **Mountain Dew** was Appalachian slang for **moonshine**; the early packaging used hillbilly bootlegger imagery.
5. **Moxie** is America's oldest continuously produced soft drink (1876), sold as "Moxie Nerve Food". The word **moxie**, meaning nerve or courage, entered English **from the drink** (first recorded 1930).
6. **Tab Clear was designed to fail.** Coca-Cola's marketing chief **Sergio Zyman** later admitted it was a "kamikaze" mission to poison the whole clear-cola category and sink **Crystal Pepsi**.
7. **New Coke** lasted **79 days** before the original returned as "Coca-Cola Classic" in 1985.
8. **Irn-Bru** outsells Coca-Cola in Scotland. Only **three people** know the formula; a copy sits in a **Swiss bank vault**.
9. **Thums Up** exists because Coca-Cola left India in 1977 over FERA. Coke bought it back in 1993 when it held **85%** of the market.
10. **Faygo** is short for the founders' surname, **Feigenson** — two Russian-Jewish bakers who flavoured soda from **cake-frosting recipes**.

## Patent-medicine beginnings

Most famous sodas began as pharmacy products, not refreshments.

| Drink | Medicinal claim |
| --- | --- |
| **Coca-Cola** | Invented 1886 by Atlanta pharmacist **John Pemberton** as a "brain tonic", partly aimed at weaning morphine addicts |
| **Pepsi** | Named for **dyspepsia**; sold by pharmacist Caleb Bradham as a digestive aid |
| **7 Up** | Contained **lithium citrate** — still a psychiatric medicine — until 1948 |
| **Moxie** | Sold as "Moxie Nerve Food", claiming to cure insomnia and "softening of the brain" |
| **Dr Pepper** | Created at a Waco drugstore in 1885, a year before Coca-Cola |

## Name origins

| Drink | Origin |
| --- | --- |
| **Fanta** | German *Fantasie*. Improvised by Max Keith's team from **whey** (a cheese byproduct) and **apple pomace** under wartime rationing. Atlanta had no contact with Keith for the whole war and did not know until it ended whether he had stayed loyal — he had. |
| **Mountain Dew** | Appalachian slang for **moonshine** |
| **Thums Up** | The "b" was dropped from "Thumbs Up" so the name could be **trademarked** |
| **Irn-Bru** | Originally "Iron Brew", changed because it is not brewed and barely contains iron |
| **Faygo** | Short for **Feigenson**, the founding brothers |
| **Moxie** | The drink came first; the English word for nerve came from it |

## The famous flops

### Crystal Pepsi and Tab Clear

Crystal Pepsi launched in **1992** on the "clear = pure" wave that also gave the year clear dish soap and clear deodorant. It worked at first: about 1% market share, a Super Bowl ad, a "Best New Product" award.

Coca-Cola's answer, **Tab Clear**, was **deliberately engineered to be bad**. Marketing chief Sergio Zyman admitted years later that the goal was never to compete but to confuse the category — launching under the diet-coded "Tab" name so that Crystal Pepsi would be read as a diet gimmick by association. Early reviews called Tab Clear cinnamony and peppery; not tasting like cola was the point. Both were dead within about five months of each other. Zyman called it a **kamikaze** mission: a company sacrificing one of its own products purely to sink a rival's bet.

### New Coke

Coca-Cola reformulated its 99-year-old recipe in **April 1985** after blind taste tests favoured a sweeter mix. The backlash — protest hotlines, "Bring Back Old Coke" clubs, hoarding — forced the original back as **Coca-Cola Classic** after **79 days**. It is the standard business-school case on underestimating brand loyalty.

### Schlitz

Once the **best-selling beer on earth** (1934), on the slogan "the beer that made Milwaukee famous". In the 1970s management swapped malted barley for **corn syrup**, moved to **accelerated batch fermentation** and added **silica gel** to control haze — which instead left visible **floaters** in the beer. Drinkers abandoned it. The 1977 ad campaign nicknamed "**Drink Schlitz or I'll kill you**", featuring tough guys threatening critics, made it worse. Schlitz was **permanently discontinued in May 2026**, with a farewell batch brewed from the original 1948 recipe.

## Oddities

| Drink | Hook |
| --- | --- |
| **Corona** | A 2020 PR-firm survey found **38%** of Americans polled would not buy it "under any circumstances" because of the coronavirus name — despite no connection beyond the Latin for **crown**. |
| **Faygo and the Juggalos** | Insane Clown Posse's Violent J once threw an open Faygo bottle at a heckler; the crowd loved it, and the "**Faygo Shower**" became a ritual. Faygo has **never officially endorsed ICP**, not least after the FBI's 2011 "hybrid gang" classification of Juggalos. |
| **Vernors** | Detroit's ginger ale, aged in oak — the barrel-aging story is tied to its founder's Civil War service. |
