Fifth in the Christ Com prep set: stock-market, economy and deal jargon, each with a one-line meaning and the origin story a quizmaster would ask about.

## Quiz-ready

If you only have ten minutes, revise these. Each one is a "who coined it" or "named after what" question.

1. **Greenshoe**: the Green Shoe Manufacturing Company (1963). **Red herring**: the red-ink warning on the prospectus.
2. **Blue chip**: poker chips. **Bellwether**: the belled ram. **Tenbagger**: Peter Lynch, from baseball.
3. **Bull and bear**: horns up, paws down. The *Charging Bull* was guerrilla art.
4. **Dead cat bounce**: *Financial Times*, 1985.
5. **Stagflation**: Iain Macleod, 1965. **Shrinkflation**: Pippa Malmgren (and Toblerone's gaps).
6. **Big Mac index**: *The Economist*, 1986. India's version uses the **Maharaja Mac**. **Lipstick index**: Leonard Lauder. **Hemline index**: George Taylor.
7. **Laffer curve**: drawn on a napkin. **Phillips**: also built the water-powered MONIAC.
8. **Animal spirits**: Keynes. **Irrational exuberance**: Greenspan. **Invisible hand**: Adam Smith (it appears only once).
9. **Black swan**: Taleb. **Grey rhino**: Wucker. **BRIC**: Jim O'Neill. **Hindu rate of growth**: Raj Krishna.
10. **Unicorn**: Aileen Lee (2013). **Poison pill**: Martin Lipton. **Pac-Man defence**: the video game.
11. **Black Monday** (1987), **Black Wednesday** (1992, Soros), **Black Tuesday** (1929), **Black Friday** (1869, gold).
12. **Bourse**: the Van der Beurze inn, Bruges. **Wall Street**: the Dutch wall. **NYSE**: buttonwood tree. **BSE**: banyan tree.
13. **Sensex** = sensitive index (coined by Deepak Mohoni). **Nifty** = National + Fifty; base date 3 Nov 1995.
14. **Cobra effect**: British Delhi's cobra bounty. **Dutch disease**: *The Economist*, 1977.
15. **2026**: Jio's IPO had no offer for sale; SpaceX made the largest IPO ever; muhurat trading is on 8 Nov in the afternoon.
16. **₹ symbol**: D. Udaya Kumar, IIT Bombay (2010).

## IPOs and capital raising

The quiz-worthy ones carry a hidden story: the **greenshoe** is named after a shoe company and the **red herring** after red ink. 2026 gave a live example of a large IPO with no offer for sale: Jio Platforms' ₹30,213 crore issue was all new shares.

| Term | Meaning | Origin / quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **IPO** (initial public offering) | A company sells shares to the public for the first time and lists on an exchange. | 2026's records: SpaceX (Nasdaq, largest IPO ever) and Jio Platforms (India's largest). |
| **Fresh issue** | New shares are created and the money goes to the company. | Jio's IPO was entirely a fresh issue. |
| **OFS** (offer for sale) | Existing shareholders (promoters, investors) sell their shares; the money goes to them, not the company. | The government also uses an "OFS through the stock exchange window" to sell stakes in public-sector companies. |
| **FPO** (follow-on public offer) | A listed company issues more shares to the public. | Vodafone Idea's ₹18,000 crore FPO (2024) was India's largest. Adani Enterprises withdrew a fully subscribed FPO in 2023 after the Hindenburg report. |
| **DRHP / RHP** | Draft red herring prospectus and red herring prospectus: the offer document filed with SEBI before the price is final. | The **red herring** name comes from the red-ink warning on its cover saying the details may change. OYO's parent PRISM filed confidentially. |
| **Book building** | Investors bid within a price band, and the final price is discovered from demand. | The alternative is a fixed-price issue. |
| **Price band / cut-off price** | The range bids must fall within; retail investors can bid at the "cut-off" (the final price). | — |
| **Anchor investors** | Large institutions given shares a day before the IPO opens, with a lock-in. | Jio's anchor book was ₹8,110 crore. |
| **QIB / NII / RII** | Qualified institutional buyers / non-institutional (HNI) investors / retail individual investors. | The three IPO quotas in India. |
| **Subscription** | How many times the shares on offer were bid for (e.g. "22 times"). | — |
| **GMP** (grey market premium) | The unofficial premium at which IPO shares trade before listing. | Unregulated, but widely watched. |
| **Listing gain / pop** | The rise on the first trading day over the issue price. | Jio listed at a 21.4% premium. |
| **Greenshoe option** | Lets underwriters sell up to 15% more shares than planned, to steady the price after listing. | Named after the **Green Shoe Manufacturing Company** (later Stride Rite), the first to use it in 1963. |
| **Lock-in** | A period when promoters or anchor investors can't sell. | — |
| **ASBA** | Application Supported by Blocked Amount: the money is only blocked in your bank account until allotment. | Used together with UPI mandates for Indian IPOs. |
| **QIP** (qualified institutional placement) | A listed company quickly raises money from institutions only. | India's fast fundraising route since 2006. |
| **Rights issue** | Existing shareholders get the right to buy new shares, usually at a discount. | Reliance's ₹53,124 crore rights issue in 2020 was India's largest. |
| **Bonus issue / stock split** | Free extra shares / one share divided into several. Neither changes the company's value. | — |
| **Buyback** | The company buys back its own shares. | — |
| **Delisting** | Removing shares from the exchange. | Reverse book building decides the exit price in India. |
| **SPAC** | Special purpose acquisition company: a listed "blank-cheque" shell that buys a private company. | A US boom in 2020–21. |
| **Direct listing** | Listing without issuing new shares or using underwriters. | Spotify (2018) and Slack. |
| **SME IPO** | A small-company IPO on the BSE SME or NSE Emerge platforms. | Some tiny issues drew bids thousands of times over, which led SEBI to tighten the rules. |

## Trading and market slang

Market slang is mostly animals, colours and days of the week. The colours and days each come with one famous event to remember: blue chips come from poker, Black Monday is 1987, and Black Wednesday is George Soros.

### Animals, chips and bags

| Term | Meaning | Origin / quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bull / bear market** | A rising / falling market (a bear market is usually a 20% fall from the peak). | A bull thrusts its horns up and a bear swipes its paws down. "Bearskin jobbers" in 18th-century London sold "the bear's skin before catching the bear", meaning they sold shares they didn't own. Wall Street's *Charging Bull* (Arturo Di Modica, 1989) was installed illegally as guerrilla art. *Fearless Girl* (2017) was a State Street ad. |
| **Stag** | Someone who buys IPO shares to flip them quickly. | — |
| **Dead cat bounce** | A brief recovery during a fall. | "Even a dead cat will bounce if it falls from a great height." It first appeared in the *Financial Times* in 1985. |
| **Blue chip** | A large, reliable company. | **Poker's** highest-value chips are blue. The term is credited to Dow Jones's Oliver Gingold in the 1920s. |
| **Penny stock** | A very cheap, often speculative share. | — |
| **Bellwether** | A stock or sector that shows which way the market will go. | The **wether** was the castrated ram wearing a bell that led the flock. |
| **Multibagger / tenbagger** | A stock that multiplies in value. | Fund manager **Peter Lynch** coined "tenbagger" from baseball, where a "bag" is a base. |
| **Bagholder** | Someone stuck holding a stock that has crashed. | — |
| **HODL** | Hold on to an investment, especially crypto. | A misspelt "I AM HODLING" post on the Bitcointalk forum in 2013. |
| **Diamond hands / to the moon / stonks / YOLO** | Reddit (WallStreetBets) slang. | From the 2021 GameStop squeeze, the subject of *Dumb Money*. |

### Moves and tactics

| Term | Meaning | Origin / quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Short selling** | Selling borrowed shares, hoping to buy them back cheaper. | **Hindenburg Research** (named after the 1937 airship disaster) shorted Adani in 2023 and shut down in Jan 2025. |
| **Short squeeze** | Short sellers are forced to buy back as the price rises, pushing it higher. | GameStop (2021); Volkswagen briefly became the world's most valuable company in 2008. |
| **Circuit breaker / upper and lower circuit** | Trading halts when an index or a stock moves too far in a day. | Introduced in the US after **Black Monday**. In India, index-wide breakers trigger at 10%, 15% and 20%. |
| **Pump and dump** | Hyping a stock, then selling it. | — |
| **Front running** | Trading ahead of a client's big order. | — |
| **Insider trading** | Trading on unpublished price-sensitive information. | Rajat Gupta (ex-McKinsey) was convicted in the US in 2012. |
| **Window dressing** | Fund managers buying winners just before reporting dates to look good. | — |
| **Margin call** | A demand for more money when a leveraged position falls. | Also the title of a 2011 film about the 2008 crash. |
| **Futures / options (F&O)** | Contracts to buy or sell later at a fixed price / the right, but not the obligation, to do so. | SEBI found about 9 in 10 Indian retail F&O traders lose money, and tightened rules in 2024. |
| **Triple witching** | The day when stock options, index futures and index options all expire. | — |
| **VIX** | Volatility index, the "fear gauge". | From Chicago's CBOE. India has its own India VIX. |

### Colours, days and places

| Term | What happened | Hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Black Friday (1869)** | Jay Gould and James Fisk tried to corner the US gold market. | Today's shopping "Black Friday" is unrelated. |
| **Black Tuesday** | 29 Oct 1929, the Wall Street Crash. | Led into the Great Depression. |
| **Black Monday** | 19 Oct 1987: the Dow fell 22.6% in a day. | Still the largest one-day percentage fall. |
| **Black Wednesday** | 16 Sep 1992: the UK left the Exchange Rate Mechanism. | **George Soros** made about $1 bn and became "the man who broke the Bank of England". |
| **Flash crash** | 6 May 2010: US stocks plunged and recovered within minutes. | Blamed partly on a UK trader spoofing from his parents' house. |
| **Wall Street** | New York's financial district. | Named after a wall built by the Dutch settlers of New Amsterdam. |
| **Buttonwood Agreement** | 1792: the founding of the NYSE. | 24 brokers signed it under a **buttonwood tree**. Pair it with the BSE's start under a **banyan tree** (see the India section). |
| **Bourse** | A stock exchange. | From the **Van der Beurze** family's inn in Bruges, where merchants met. |
| **First stock exchange** | Amsterdam, 1602. | Set up to trade shares of the Dutch East India Company (VOC), the first publicly traded company. |
| **Ticker** | The live price feed. | From the ticking of the ticker-tape machine (1867; improved by Edison). It also gave ticker-tape parades. |

## Macroeconomics

The best economics questions ask "who coined it". Stagflation goes back to a 1965 speech in Parliament, the Big Mac index to *The Economist* in 1986, and the Laffer curve to a napkin.

### The -flations

| Term | Meaning | Origin / quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inflation / deflation** | Prices rising / falling across the economy. | — |
| **Disinflation** | Inflation is still positive but slowing. | Often confused with deflation. |
| **Reflation** | Policy that pushes prices and growth back up after a slump. | — |
| **Hyperinflation** | Extreme price rises (often 50%+ a month). | Hungary in 1946 is the worst on record. Weimar Germany (1923) and Zimbabwe (2008, a **Z$100 trillion** note) are the famous ones. |
| **Stagflation** | Stagnation plus inflation: low growth and high prices at once. | Coined by UK politician **Iain Macleod** in the House of Commons in **1965**. The 1970s oil shocks made it famous. |
| **Shrinkflation** | Smaller packs at the same price. | Popularised by economist **Pippa Malmgren**. Toblerone widened the gaps between its triangles in 2016. |
| **Skimpflation / greedflation** | Worse quality or service at the same price / companies raising prices beyond their costs. | Both 2020s coinages. |

### Cycles and policy

| Term | Meaning | Origin / quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recession / depression** | Shrinking output (the rule of thumb is two quarters of falling GDP) / a long, severe one. | "A recession is when your neighbour loses his job; a depression is when you lose yours." Usually attributed to Harry Truman. |
| **V, U, W, L and K-shaped recovery** | The shape of the rebound on a chart. | "K-shaped" (2020) means the rich recover while the poor fall behind. |
| **Soft landing / hard landing** | Slowing inflation without / with a recession. | — |
| **Goldilocks economy** | Not too hot, not too cold: steady growth with low inflation. | From the fairy tale. |
| **Quantitative easing (QE)** | A central bank buys bonds to pump in money. | Japan pioneered it in 2001. |
| **Taper tantrum** | The 2013 market panic when the US Fed said it would slow QE. | It led Morgan Stanley to name the **Fragile Five**: India, Indonesia, Brazil, Turkey and South Africa. |
| **Helicopter money** | Printing money and handing it straight to the public. | Milton Friedman's 1969 image. Ben Bernanke became "Helicopter Ben". |
| **Liquidity trap** | Rate cuts stop working because people hoard cash. | Keynes. |
| **Animal spirits** | The emotions that drive economic decisions. | Keynes, *The General Theory* (1936). |
| **Irrational exuberance** | A speculative bubble. | **Alan Greenspan**, in a 1996 speech. Robert Shiller later used it as a book title. |
| **Invisible hand** | Self-interest guiding markets to the common good. | **Adam Smith**. It appears only once in *The Wealth of Nations*. |
| **Black swan / grey rhino** | A rare, unforeseeable shock / an obvious threat that gets ignored. | Nassim Nicholas **Taleb** (2007) / Michele **Wucker** (2016). |
| **BRIC** | Brazil, Russia, India, China. | Coined by Goldman Sachs economist **Jim O'Neill** in 2001. It became BRICS when South Africa joined in 2010. |
| **Hindu rate of growth** | India's slow growth of about 3.5% a year from the 1950s to the 1980s. | Coined by economist **Raj Krishna**. |
| **Gross National Happiness** | A measure of wellbeing instead of GDP. | **Bhutan**. |

### Indexes and laws named after things

| Term | Meaning | Origin / quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Big Mac index** | Compares currencies using the price of a Big Mac (purchasing power parity). | Started by ***The Economist*** in **1986** as a joke, and nicknamed "Burgernomics". India has no beef burger, so it uses the chicken **Maharaja Mac**. |
| **Lipstick index** | Lipstick sales rise in recessions, as people trade down to small luxuries. | **Leonard Lauder** of Estée Lauder, after 2001 (see the cosmetics doc). |
| **Hemline index** | Skirts get shorter in booms. | Economist **George Taylor**, 1926. |
| **Skyscraper index** | Record-tall buildings get finished just before crashes. | Andrew Lawrence, 1999. |
| **Misery index** | Inflation plus unemployment. | **Arthur Okun**, 1970s. |
| **Laffer curve** | Past a certain point, higher tax rates bring in less revenue. | Economist Arthur Laffer drew it on a **napkin** in 1974 for Cheney and Rumsfeld. The napkin is in the Smithsonian. |
| **Phillips curve** | Unemployment and inflation trade off against each other. | A.W. **Phillips**, who also built the **MONIAC**, a computer of the economy that ran on flowing water. |
| **Gini coefficient / Lorenz curve** | Measures of inequality. | Corrado Gini (Italy) and Max Lorenz. |
| **Gresham's law** | "Bad money drives out good." | Sir Thomas Gresham, Elizabeth I's financier, who founded the Royal Exchange. |
| **Veblen goods** | Luxuries people want more of as the price rises. | Thorstein Veblen, who also coined "conspicuous consumption". |
| **Giffen goods** | Staples people buy more of as the price rises, because they can't afford anything better. | — |
| **Cobra effect** | A fix that makes the problem worse. | British Delhi paid a bounty on dead cobras, so people bred cobras. |
| **Dutch disease** | A resource boom hurts manufacturing by strengthening the currency. | ***The Economist*** (1977), after the Netherlands' gas discovery. |
| **Tragedy of the commons** | Shared resources get overused. | Garrett Hardin, 1968. |
| **Broken window fallacy** | Destruction doesn't create net wealth. | Frédéric Bastiat, 1850. |

### Bubbles and frauds

| Term | What happened | Hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tulip mania** | Dutch tulip-bulb bubble, 1637. | Often called the first speculative bubble. |
| **South Sea Bubble** | 1720, London. | Isaac Newton lost a fortune: "I can calculate the motions of heavenly bodies, but not the madness of people." |
| **Mississippi Bubble** | 1720, France. | Scotsman John Law's paper-money scheme. |
| **Ponzi scheme** | Paying old investors with new investors' money. | **Charles Ponzi**, 1920, using international postal reply coupons. Bernie Madoff ran the biggest one. |

## Deals, takeovers and start-up terms

Takeover jargon is a 1980s Wall Street bestiary: knights, sharks, parachutes and a video game. Start-up jargon is a zoo: unicorns, centaurs, camels and, fittingly for 2026, cockroaches.

### Takeovers

| Term | Meaning | Origin / quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hostile takeover / bear hug** | Buying a company against its board's wishes / an offer so generous the board can hardly refuse. | — |
| **Poison pill** | Letting other shareholders buy cheap shares to dilute a raider. | Invented by lawyer **Martin Lipton** (1982). Twitter adopted one against Elon Musk in 2022. |
| **White knight / white squire** | A friendly buyer who rescues the target / a friendly investor who takes only a stake. | Grey and black knights are the less friendly versions. |
| **Pac-Man defence** | The target makes a counter-bid for the attacker. | Named after the **video game**. Used in the 1982 Bendix–Martin Marietta battle. |
| **Crown jewel defence / scorched earth** | Selling the target's most valuable assets / making the whole company unattractive. | — |
| **Shark repellent** | Anti-takeover clauses in the company's charter. | — |
| **Greenmail** | Buying back a raider's stake at a premium to make it go away. | Green (dollars) plus blackmail. |
| **Dawn raid** | Buying a big stake quickly when the market opens. | — |
| **Golden parachute / handshake / handcuffs** | A big payout for executives who lose their jobs in a takeover / a generous retirement payout / perks that make leaving costly. | — |
| **Golden share** | A government's special share that can veto changes in a privatised company. | Used in UK privatisations. |
| **LBO** (leveraged buyout) | Buying a company with mostly borrowed money. | KKR's RJR Nabisco deal (1988) inspired the book and film *Barbarians at the Gate*. |
| **Open offer** | In India, buying past the takeover threshold forces an offer to all public shareholders. | SEBI's takeover code sets the trigger at 25%. |
| **Demerger / spin-off** | Splitting a business into a new listed company. | ITC Hotels (2025) and Tata Motors' passenger and commercial vehicle split (2025). |
| **Chinese wall** | An information barrier inside a firm, e.g. between bankers and traders. | Now usually called an "ethical wall". |

### Start-ups and venture capital

| Term | Meaning | Origin / quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unicorn** | A private start-up worth $1 bn or more. | Coined by **Aileen Lee** of Cowboy Ventures in 2013. |
| **Decacorn / hectocorn** | Worth $10 bn / $100 bn or more. | SpaceX was the classic hectocorn before its IPO. |
| **Soonicorn / minicorn** | Close to unicorn status / a smaller high-value start-up. | Soonicorn is common in Indian media (Inc42's tracker). |
| **Centaur** | A start-up with $100 m in annual recurring revenue. | — |
| **Camel / cockroach** | A start-up built to survive lean times. | Topical in 2026 because of the "cockroach" theme. |
| **Zombie** | A company that earns just enough to service its debt but can't grow. | — |
| **Burn rate / runway** | Cash spent per month / months of cash left. | — |
| **Down round / flat round** | Raising money at a lower / the same valuation as before. | Byju's fall is the Indian example. |
| **Angel investor** | An early individual backer. | The term came from Broadway, where wealthy patrons funded shows. |
| **Bootstrapping** | Building a company without outside money. | From "pulling yourself up by your bootstraps". Zerodha is India's poster child. |
| **Product-market fit / MVP / pivot** | Real demand for the product / a minimum viable product / a change of direction. | "Product-market fit" was popularised by **Marc Andreessen** (2007). |
| **Blitzscaling** | Chasing growth over efficiency. | **Reid Hoffman** (LinkedIn). |
| **Acqui-hire** | Buying a company mainly for its people. | — |
| **ESOP / vesting / cliff** | Employee stock options / earning them over time / the minimum wait before any vest. | — |
| **ARR, GMV, CAC, LTV** | Annual recurring revenue; gross merchandise value; customer acquisition cost; customer lifetime value. | — |
| **"Move fast and break things"** | Facebook's early motto. | Changed in 2014 to "Move fast with stable infrastructure". |

## Indian markets

Two dates matter this week. The Nifty's base date is **3 November 1995**, so it turns 31 on the day you read this. This year's Diwali **muhurat trading** session is on **8 November 2026**, unusually in the afternoon (2:20–3:30 pm).

### Exchanges and indices

| Term | Meaning | Origin / quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dalal Street** | Mumbai's financial district, home of the BSE. | *Dalal* means broker. The BSE's tower is **Phiroze Jeejeebhoy Towers**, named after a former BSE chairman. |
| **BSE** | Asia's oldest stock exchange, formalised in 1875 as the Native Share & Stock Brokers' Association. | Brokers first met under a **banyan tree** near Bombay's Town Hall in the 1850s. That pairs with the NYSE's buttonwood tree. Premchand Roychand, the "Cotton King", was a founder. |
| **Sensex** | BSE's 30-stock index; the name is short for "sensitive index". | The name was coined by market analyst **Deepak Mohoni**. Base year 1978–79 = 100; launched in 1986. |
| **NSE / Nifty 50** | National Stock Exchange (1992), India's first screen-based exchange / its 50-stock index. | **Nifty** = **N**ational + **Fifty**. Base date 3 Nov 1995 = 1,000. |
| **GIFT Nifty** | Nifty futures traded at GIFT City, Gandhinagar. | It used to be SGX Nifty in Singapore and moved to GIFT City in July 2023. |
| **Muhurat trading** | A one-hour Diwali session on the first day of the new Samvat year. | 2026: 8 Nov, 2:20–3:30 pm. |
| **T+1 / T+0** | Shares settle one day after the trade / on the same day. | India completed the move to **T+1** in January 2023, before the US. Optional T+0 began in 2024. |
| **Demat** | Shares held electronically. | Two depositories: **NSDL** (1996) and **CDSL** (1999). |
| **SEBI** | Securities and Exchange Board of India: set up in 1988, given legal powers in 1992 after the Harshad Mehta scam. | — |
| **Promoter** | India's word for the founders or controlling shareholders. | — |
| **FPI / DII** | Foreign portfolio investors / domestic institutional investors (mutual funds, LIC). | In recent years, domestic SIP money has often outweighed foreign selling. |
| **SIP / NAV / NFO** | Systematic investment plan / net asset value / new fund offer. | AMFI's "Mutual Funds Sahi Hai" campaign began in 2017. |
| **Badla** | The BSE's old system for carrying trades forward to the next settlement. | Banned in 2001 after the Ketan Parekh scam. |

### RBI and money

| Term | Meaning | Hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Repo rate** | The rate at which the RBI lends to banks overnight. | Set by the **Monetary Policy Committee**: six members, created in 2016, with an inflation target of 4% ± 2%. |
| **Reverse repo / SDF** | The RBI paying banks to park money. | Since 2022 the **Standing Deposit Facility** has been the floor rate instead. |
| **MSF / bank rate** | Emergency borrowing for banks; the long-term lending rate. | — |
| **CRR / SLR** | Cash reserve ratio (held with the RBI) / statutory liquidity ratio (held in government bonds, gold or cash). | — |
| **₹ symbol** | Designed by **D. Udaya Kumar** in 2010. | It blends the Devanagari "Ra" and the Roman "R". He was then a postgraduate at **IIT Bombay**'s IDC. |
| **Hawala / benami** | Informal money transfer / property held in someone else's name. | — |
| **GST 2.0** | From Sep 2025, mostly two GST slabs (5% and 18%) plus a 40% rate for sin and luxury goods. | — |

### Indian scams that coined vocabulary

| Scam | Year | Hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Harshad Mehta** | 1992 | Bank-receipt fraud; led to SEBI getting statutory powers; the series *Scam 1992*. |
| **Ketan Parekh** | 2001 | Circular trading in "K-10" stocks; led to the end of *badla*. |
| **Satyam** | 2009 | Ramalinga Raju confessed to faking about ₹7,000 crore of cash; called "India's Enron". |
| **Hindenburg vs Adani** | 2023 | The short-seller's report knocked over $100 bn off the group's value. |
