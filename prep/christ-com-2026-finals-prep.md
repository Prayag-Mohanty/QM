## What the prelims tell us

The [prelims](https://prayag-mohanty.github.io/QM/quiz/christ-com-quiz-2026-prelims-by-major-chandrakant-nair/) were 16 questions, and almost every answer is a well-known brand or name reached through an unusual door: a trademark fight, a pun in a company name, a colour, a film. **Patterns that matter for the finals**

- **Trademarks and colours came up three times** (T-Mobile magenta, Cadbury purple, the Modi kurta). That is the clearest repeat theme.
- **Name puns and eponyms came up four times** (Listerine, Smirnoff, TGIF, 1492). Expect more "why is it called that" questions.
- **There was a host-college question.** Q14 hinged on a Christ College alumnus. Expect Thrissur, Kerala and alumni business in the finals.
- **Visual and connect questions** (Thar, Mondrian, the Allahabad Bank logo) suggest a finals with image rounds.
- **Difficulty was moderate.** None of the 16 needs obscure recall; the finals will add depth, but the style rewards working out an answer from the clue.

## The quizmaster and the event

**Major Chandrakant Nair** is a former army doctor turned full-time quizmaster, a [former World Quizzing Champion](https://www.ted.com/talks/chandrakant_nair_the_question_of_a_question), and a long-time name on the [Kerala circuit](http://greycells-kerala.blogspot.com/2007/08/chandrakant-nair.html). He is known for a calm style and deeply researched, thought-provoking questions; his TEDx talk is literally about how he builds a question. Watch it before the finals: it is the closest thing to a briefing on his method.

To calibrate on his finals-length style, play one of his recorded general quizzes on YouTube, such as the [IIT (ISM) Dhanbad Excelsior'21 GQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j84UXl2eOWk). They are general rather than business quizzes, but the question construction carries over.

**Christ Com Quiz** is the Commerce Department's all-India intercollegiate quiz at Christ College (Autonomous), Irinjalakuda, in Thrissur district. The [first edition, on 9 February 2024](https://christcommerce.com/submenudisplay/CHRIST%20COM%20QUIZ), was won by FMS Delhi, with IIT Madras second and IIM Kozhikode third, so expect a strong field. That edition was inaugurated by the managing director of KPL Oil Mills, a local Irinjalakuda company; sponsor and host-college questions are fair game.

**Unknowns to settle before the day:** the finals format, how many teams reach the stage, whether there is a written elimination, and whether pounce, bounce and negative marking apply. Ask the organisers or check the registration message; the answer changes how aggressive you should be (see tactics).

**Thrissur's banking story** is another strong local seam. In the 1930s Thrissur held the head offices of 58 banks, and it is still home to [four scheduled banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Thrissur): South Indian Bank (1929), CSB (1920), Dhanlaxmi (1927) and ESAF (2017).

## Six-day plan

The study list above is day one. The other five days are about volume, which is what wins circuit finals: full sets played under time, a three-month news backlog cleared, whole reference lists read rather than highlights, and questions written in Major Nair's style. **Targets by Wednesday night: 10 full sets played, about 200 logged misses, 100 self-written questions between you, and one mock final.**

**Every day has the same shape (about 5.5 hours):**

| Block | Time | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| News | 1 h, morning | One slice of the news backlog each, separately; swap your 10 most quizzable items |
| Deep | 2 h | That day's theme, split between you as below |
| Practice | 1.5 h | Two full sets under real timing, played as a pair |
| Evening | 1 h | Swap 10 self-written questions each; log every miss |

**Where the practice sets come from:** the six BBQL Season 3 weeks in your business quiz guide; your own QM archive (the Zerodha Varsity 2026 Mumbai prelims, the Quizzinga Biz & Innovation quiz); past Tata Crucible and circuit business finals on SlideShare and YouTube; and Major Nair's recorded quizzes for style.

**The mock final (Tuesday):** ask a friend from your quiz club to read 40 questions you haven't seen, mixing both your question banks with one unplayed set. Play it under the finals rules once the organisers confirm them, including pounce and negatives. Review it the same night.

**The miss log:** one shared sheet, with a row per miss: question, answer, theme and two related facts. By Wednesday it is the only thing you revise.

## Finals-day tactics

- **Work the angle, not just your memory.** Major Nair's prelims answers were familiar names behind unusual clues. When stuck, ask why this fact would make a good question: a pun, a colour, a trademark, a film, a local link.
- **Divide the listening.** One partner tracks the clue chain and writes candidates; the other checks the most specific detail against them. Swap roles by round so neither tires.
- **Agree a confidence signal.** Use a simple scale (sure, likely, guess) before every answer, so the decision to pounce or pass takes one glance.
- **Read the room.** Note the sponsors on banners, the event posters and the college's history. Host and sponsor questions are common, and the prelims already had one.
- **Respect negatives late.** If negative marking applies, a wrong answer in the last round costs more than a pass when you lead. Pounce only when you are clearly confident and the lead is at stake.
- **For connects and visuals,** name each element out loud first, then look for the link: a person, a colour, a place, a brand, an era.
- **Give the answer form asked for.** A full name, a legal name or the exact phrase. Spell names the way the setter would.
