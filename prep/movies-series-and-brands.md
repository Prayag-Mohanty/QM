## Product placement and brand cameos

The most askable placements are the ones that changed a brand's sales, or the ones a director hid on purpose. Fight Club and E.T. are the classics.

| Film (year) | Brand | The story | Quiz angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fight Club** (1999) | Starbucks | David Fincher said a Starbucks cup appears in nearly every scene, as a dig at consumer culture. Starbucks let him use the logo but refused to have a store smashed. The film destroys a generic coffee shop instead. | Also: the narrator's IKEA catalogue apartment ("I'd flip through catalogues and wonder, what kind of dining set defines me as a person?"), and soap made from liposuction fat. |
| **E.T.** (1982) | Reese's Pieces (Hershey) | Mars reportedly turned down the chance to use M&M's. Hershey took it, and Reese's Pieces sales reportedly jumped about 65%. | Often called the birth of modern product placement. |
| **Cast Away** (2000) | FedEx; Wilson | Tom Hanks plays a FedEx systems engineer. FedEx did not pay for the placement. His only friend is a Wilson volleyball he names "Wilson". | FedEx founder Fred Smith appears in a cameo. The unopened package he delivers at the end became a running joke. |
| **James Bond** | Aston Martin, Omega, Heineken, BMW, Bollinger | The Aston Martin DB5 debuted in *Goldfinger* (1964). Omega replaced Rolex from *GoldenEye* (1995). BMW's three-film deal began with the Z3 in *GoldenEye*. Heineken reportedly paid around $45 million for *Skyfall*, so Bond drank beer. | Connery-era Bond wore a Rolex Submariner. Bond's champagne is Bollinger. |
| **Risky Business** (1983), **Top Gun** (1986), **Men in Black** (1997) | Ray-Ban | Tom Cruise's Wayfarers revived a dying model. Top Gun did the same for the Aviator. *Men in Black* boosted the Predator. | Ray-Ban is now owned by EssilorLuxottica. |
| **Back to the Future Part II** (1989) | Nike, Pepsi | Marty's self-lacing Nike Mag, and "Pepsi Perfect" in the 2015 café. | Nike made real Mags in 2011 and self-lacing ones in 2016. Pepsi sold a limited Pepsi Perfect on 21 Oct 2015, "Back to the Future Day". |
| **The Matrix** (1999) | Nokia | Neo's spring-loaded **Nokia 8110**, nicknamed the "banana phone". | Nokia (by then HMD) re-released the 8110 in 2018. Samsung supplied the phones in the sequels. |
| **Iron Man** (2008) | Burger King, Audi | Tony Stark's first wish after escaping captivity is an American cheeseburger: Burger King. He drives an Audi R8. | Audi has been a Marvel car partner ever since. |
| **Ant-Man** (2015) | Baskin-Robbins | Scott Lang is fired from Baskin-Robbins: "Baskin-Robbins always finds out." | A placement that works as a joke. |
| **Transformers** (2007) | Chevrolet Camaro | Bumblebee is a yellow Camaro. GM revived the model alongside the film. | Michael Bay and GM shared marketing. |
| **The Italian Job** (1969 and 2003) | Mini | Both versions use a Mini Cooper heist chase. | The 2003 remake launched with BMW's new MINI. |
| **Jurassic Park** (1993) | Ford, Jeep, Mercedes | The park's tour vehicles are Ford Explorers and Jeep Wranglers. | The film sells its own merchandise in the gift-shop scene, a joke about itself. |
| **Minority Report** (2002) | Lexus, Gap, Guinness | Billboards scan your eyes and greet you by name. | It predicted personalised ads. |
| **Wayne's World** (1992) | Pizza Hut, Doritos, Pepsi, Nuprin, Reebok | Wayne refuses to "sell out" while holding each product up to the camera. | The classic parody of product placement. |
| **Knives Out** (2019) | Apple | Director Rian Johnson said Apple lets characters use iPhones on screen but not villains. | That gives away who the villain isn't. |
| **You've Got Mail** (1998) | AOL, Starbucks | The romance happens over AOL email. Its title is AOL's own alert. | Fox Books is a stand-in for Barnes & Noble. |
| **Big** (1988) | FAO Schwarz | Tom Hanks plays "Heart and Soul" and "Chopsticks" on the giant floor piano. | Tourists still visit to play the piano. |
| **Breakfast at Tiffany's** (1961) | Tiffany & Co. | Audrey Hepburn eats breakfast outside the Fifth Avenue store. | Tiffany reportedly opened on a Sunday for the first time to allow filming. |
| **The Devil Wears Prada** (2006) | Prada, *Vogue* | Miranda Priestly is widely read as Anna Wintour. *Runway* stands in for *Vogue*. | The "cerulean" sweater speech explains how fashion trends trickle down. A sequel was released in 2026. |
| **Toy Story** (1995) | Mr. Potato Head, Etch A Sketch, Slinky | Real toys became characters. | Sales of Slinky Dog and Etch A Sketch jumped. |
| **Cars** (2006) | — | Lightning McQueen is named after Pixar animator Glenn McQueen, not Steve McQueen. | A neat trap question. |
| **Barbie** (2023) | Mattel | The film's pink sets were reported to have used up a supplier's stock of fluorescent pink paint (Rosco). | Barbie and Ken are named after Ruth Handler's children, Barbara and Kenneth. |

## Fictional brands that became real

In the best example, the brand came before the film: Quaker Oats paid for the 1971 Wonka film to launch a chocolate bar.

| Fictional brand | From | What happened in real life |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wonka Bar** | *Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory* (1971) | Quaker Oats financed the film to launch Wonka candy. That is why the title changed from the book's *Charlie* to *Willy Wonka*. The brand later passed to Nestlé, then Ferrara. |
| **Bubba Gump Shrimp Co.** | *Forrest Gump* (1994) | Became a real seafood restaurant chain in 1996, now owned by Landry's. |
| **Soylent** | *Soylent Green* (1973) | A real meal-replacement drink launched in 2014 under the name, despite the film's twist that Soylent Green is made from people. |
| **Red Swingline stapler** | *Office Space* (1999) | Swingline didn't make a red stapler; the props team painted one. Demand after the film made Swingline sell a red model in 2002. |
| **Duff Beer** | *The Simpsons* | Fox fought unlicensed Duffs. An official Duff now sells at Universal Studios. |
| **Kwik-E-Mart** | *The Simpsons* | In 2007, 7-Eleven turned about a dozen US stores into Kwik-E-Marts for *The Simpsons Movie*, selling Buzz Cola, Squishees and pink doughnuts. |
| **Dunder Mifflin** | *The Office* (US) | Branded copy paper was sold for real under licence. Fans still order it. |
| **Los Pollos Hermanos** | *Breaking Bad* / *Better Call Saul* | Pop-up restaurants ran to promote *Better Call Saul*. |
| **Central Perk** | *Friends* | Warner Bros opened a pop-up Central Perk in New York for the show's 20th anniversary (2014), and permanent cafés have followed. |
| **Pizza Planet** | *Toy Story* | It is a restaurant at Disney parks. The Pizza Planet truck is hidden in almost every Pixar film. |
| **Nakatomi Plaza** | *Die Hard* (1988) | A real building in disguise: Fox Plaza, 20th Century Fox's own headquarters in Los Angeles. |
| **Big Kahuna Burger, Red Apple cigarettes** | Tarantino films | Fictional brands that recur across his films. Red Apple shows up in *Pulp Fiction*, *Kill Bill* and *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*. |
| **Oceanic Airlines** | *Lost* and many other shows | A stock fictional airline that Hollywood uses instead of naming a real carrier in crash stories. |
| **Acme Corporation** | Looney Tunes | Wile E. Coyote's supplier of choice. "Acme" means "the peak", and it was a common real company name. |
| **Stark Industries, Wayne Enterprises, Weyland-Yutani, Cyberdyne** | Marvel, DC, *Alien*, *Terminator* | Fictional corporations that often turn up in "top fictional companies" rankings, such as Forbes's Fictional 15. |

## Films about real companies and business people

Biopics of brands come up often because each one carries a famous line, a real deal and a casting fact. 2023 alone brought *Air*, *BlackBerry*, *Tetris*, *Flamin' Hot* and *Barbie*.

| Film (year) | Company / person | Who plays whom | Trivia worth knowing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Founder** (2016) | McDonald's, Ray Kroc | Michael Keaton as Kroc | The McDonald brothers' "Speedee Service System". Kroc bought them out for $2.7 million and later opened a McDonald's that put their own original restaurant out of business. |
| **Air** (2023) | Nike, Michael Jordan | Ben Affleck as Phil Knight; Matt Damon as Sonny Vaccaro | Jordan's mother Deloris won him a share of every Air Jordan sold, which was unheard of then. Jordan himself is never shown clearly on screen. |
| **The Social Network** (2010) | Facebook | Jesse Eisenberg as Zuckerberg | Sean Parker's advice: "Drop the 'The'. Just Facebook." One actor, Armie Hammer, played both Winklevoss twins, with a body double. |
| **Steve Jobs** (2015), **Pirates of Silicon Valley** (1999) | Apple | Michael Fassbender; Noah Wyle | Both feature the "1984" Macintosh ad, directed by Ridley Scott. |
| **BlackBerry** (2023) | Research In Motion | Jay Baruchel; Glenn Howerton | RIM was based in Waterloo, Ontario. The film shows the iPhone ending its lead. |
| **Tetris** (2023) | Nintendo, Elorg | Taron Egerton as Henk Rogers | A Cold War licensing chase for the Game Boy rights. |
| **Flamin' Hot** (2023) | Frito-Lay (Cheetos) | Directed by Eva Longoria | Richard Montañez's claim to have invented Flamin' Hot Cheetos is disputed by Frito-Lay. |
| **Ford v Ferrari** (2019) | Ford, Ferrari, Le Mans 1966 | Matt Damon as Carroll Shelby; Christian Bale as Ken Miles | Henry Ford II wanted revenge after Enzo Ferrari backed out of a deal to sell to Ford. The GT40 went on to win Le Mans. |
| **House of Gucci** (2021) | Gucci | Lady Gaga as Patrizia Reggiani | Patrizia hired a hitman to kill Maurizio Gucci in 1995. She was nicknamed the "Black Widow". |
| **Joy** (2015) | QVC, the Miracle Mop | Jennifer Lawrence as Joy Mangano | A self-wringing mop sold on home shopping TV. |
| **Jerry Maguire** (1996) | Reebok | Tom Cruise; Cuba Gooding Jr. | "Show me the money!" Reebok sued Sony because a promised Reebok ad scene was cut from the cinema release. |
| **Wall Street** (1987) | Gordon Gekko | Michael Douglas | "Greed is good" was modelled on a 1986 speech by the arbitrageur Ivan Boesky. |
| **The Wolf of Wall Street** (2013) | Stratton Oakmont, Jordan Belfort | Leonardo DiCaprio | "Sell me this pen." The real Belfort makes a cameo. |
| **The Big Short** (2015) | The 2008 mortgage crash | Christian Bale as Michael Burry | Margot Robbie explains subprime mortgages from a bubble bath. |
| **Dumb Money** (2023) | GameStop short squeeze | Paul Dano as Keith Gill, "Roaring Kitty" | Reddit's WallStreetBets against hedge fund Melvin Capital. |
| **Moneyball** (2011) | Oakland Athletics | Brad Pitt as Billy Beane | Data-driven baseball. "Moneyball" became business slang for using data to find undervalued talent. |
| **Super Size Me** (2004) | McDonald's | Morgan Spurlock | Shortly after it premiered, McDonald's dropped its Super Size option. |
| **Unfrosted** (2024) | Kellogg's vs Post, Pop-Tarts | Jerry Seinfeld | A comedy about the 1960s breakfast-pastry race. |
| **Barbie** (2023) | Mattel | Margot Robbie; Will Ferrell as Mattel's CEO | The year's biggest film worldwide. Mattel now runs Mattel Films. |
| **WeCrashed** (2022, series) | WeWork, Adam Neumann | Jared Leto; Anne Hathaway | WeWork's failed 2019 IPO. |
| **The Dropout** (2022, series) | Theranos, Elizabeth Holmes | Amanda Seyfried | Holmes was jailed for fraud. Her black turtlenecks copied Steve Jobs. |
| **Super Pumped** (2022, series) | Uber, Travis Kalanick | Joseph Gordon-Levitt | Narrated by Quentin Tarantino. |
| **The Playlist** (2022, series) | Spotify, Daniel Ek | Edvin Endre | A Swedish Netflix drama in which each episode tells the story from a different person's view. |

## TV series with brand connections

Series moved sales more than films did: Squid Game sent Vans slip-on sales up about 7,800%, and Stranger Things brought back New Coke.

| Series | Brand link | The story | Quiz angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mad Men** (2007–15) | Lucky Strike, Kodak, Coca-Cola | Don Draper's "It's toasted" pitch uses Lucky Strike's real slogan (1917). He calls Kodak's slide projector the "Carousel", which was its real name. | The finale ends on Coca-Cola's real 1971 "Hilltop" ad, "I'd like to buy the world a Coke", implying Don wrote it. |
| **Succession** (2018–23) | Waystar Royco | A family media empire often compared with the Murdochs'. | It made "quiet luxury" a trend: Kendall's Loro Piana caps. It also gave us the "ludicrously capacious bag" joke about a Burberry tote. |
| **Silicon Valley** (2014–19) | Hooli, Pied Piper | Hooli is a parody of Google. | HBO released the show's "Not Hotdog" app for real. The fictional "Weissman score" for compression is now used half-seriously by engineers. |
| **Stranger Things** (2016–) | Coca-Cola, Eggo | Season 3 is set in 1985, the year of the New Coke flop. | Coca-Cola briefly brought back New Coke in 2019 for the season. Eleven's love of **Eggo** waffles boosted Kellogg's sales. |
| **Squid Game** (2021–) | Vans, dalgona | Players wear white Vans slip-ons. | Vans slip-on sales rose about 7,800% after launch. The dalgona honeycomb-candy challenge went viral worldwide. |
| **Game of Thrones** (2019) | Starbucks (not really) | A takeaway coffee cup was left in a Season 8 scene. | People assumed it was a Starbucks cup and it trended. It was actually from craft services, but Starbucks got the free publicity. Pair it with Fight Club. |
| **The Simpsons** (1989–) | Duff, Kwik-E-Mart, Disney/Fox | Springfield's fake brands. | A 1998 episode showed a "20th Century Fox, a division of Walt Disney Co." sign. Disney actually bought Fox in 2019. |
| **Seinfeld** (1989–98) | Junior Mints, PEZ, J. Peterman | Kramer drops a Junior Mint into a patient during surgery. | The Soup Nazi was based on a real New York chef, Al Yeganeh. J. Peterman was a real catalogue company. |
| **The Office** (US) (2005–13) | Dunder Mifflin, Staples | A paper company in Scranton. | Dunder Mifflin is later bought by Sabre. Its rivals are big-box chains like Staples. |
| **Breaking Bad** (2008–13) | Los Pollos Hermanos, Pontiac Aztek | Walter White drives a Pontiac Aztek, often called one of the ugliest cars ever made. | Gus's chicken chain is owned by Madrigal Elektromotoren, a fictional German conglomerate. |
| **Friends** (1994–2004) | Pottery Barn | An episode where Rachel hides that her furniture is all from Pottery Barn. | The "Rachel" haircut became a salon request around the world. |
| **Shark Tank** / **Dragons' Den** | — | Both come from a Japanese show, *Money Tigers* (2001). | Shark Tank India judges include Vineeta Singh (SUGAR) and Aman Gupta (boAt). |
| **The Apprentice** (2004–) | Donald Trump | Trump's catchphrase was "You're fired!" | The UK version is hosted by Alan Sugar of Amstrad. |
| **Halt and Catch Fire** (2014–17) | The 1980s PC industry | A Texas firm reverse-engineers an IBM PC, much like the real Compaq did. | The title is the joke name of a machine instruction that would crash the processor. |
| **The Bear** (2022–) | Mr. Beef, Chicago | The show's sandwich shop was filmed at Mr. Beef, a real Chicago Italian-beef stand. | It made Mr. Beef a tourist spot. |
| **Billions** (2016–23) | Axe Capital | Bobby Axelrod is widely read as based on hedge-fund boss Steve Cohen. | — |

## Indian and Malayalam films and series

Indian business cinema is mostly biopics in disguise: *Guru* is Dhirubhai Ambani, *Soorarai Pottru* is Captain Gopinath, and *Pranchiyettan* is every Thrissur trader who wants a Padma award.

| Title (year) | Real business / person | Trivia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Guru** (2007) | Widely read as Dhirubhai Ambani | Abhishek Bachchan's Gurukant Desai builds "Shakti Corp". Directed by Mani Ratnam. |
| **Scam 1992** (2020, series) | Harshad Mehta, the "Big Bull" | Based on Sucheta Dalal and Debashis Basu's book *The Scam*. Pratik Gandhi's line "Risk hai toh ishq hai" became a meme. Directed by Hansal Mehta, on SonyLIV. |
| **Scam 2003** (2023, series) | Abdul Karim Telgi's fake stamp-paper racket | The second season of the *Scam* franchise. |
| **The Big Bull** (2021) | Harshad Mehta again | Abhishek Bachchan played a fictionalised Mehta. |
| **Soorarai Pottru** (2020) | Captain G.R. Gopinath, Air Deccan | Based on his memoir *Simply Fly*. Suriya won the National Award. Remade in Hindi as *Sarfira* (2024) with Akshay Kumar. |
| **Pad Man** (2018) | Arunachalam Muruganantham, low-cost sanitary pads | Twinkle Khanna produced it, from her story *The Legend of Lakshmi Prasad*. |
| **Rocket Singh: Salesman of the Year** (2009) | Start-up from inside a company | Ranbir Kapoor's rebel firm is called "AYS – At Your Service". |
| **Corporate** (2006) | Cola rivalry | Madhur Bhandarkar's film echoes the pesticide-in-cola controversy of the time. |
| **Rocket Boys** (2022, series) | Homi Bhabha and Vikram Sarabhai | Also shows the founding of ISRO and the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad. |
| **TVF Pitchers** (2015, series) | Indian start-up culture | "Tu beer hai" became the start-up rallying cry. |
| **Bad Boy Billionaires** (2020, series) | Vijay Mallya, Nirav Modi, Subrata Roy | A court held back the Satyam (Ramalinga Raju) episode. |
| **Shark Tank India** (2021–) | boAt, SUGAR, Lenskart, Shaadi.com and others | Ashneer Grover (BharatPe) became a meme for "Ye sab doglapan hai". |
| **Pranchiyettan and the Saint** (2010, Malayalam) | A Thrissur rice trader | Mammootty plays Chirammal Enashu Francis, a rich Thrissur businessman desperate for respect and a Padma award. Directed by Ranjith, and a must for a Thrissur quizmaster. |
| **Varavelpu** (1989, Malayalam) | Kerala trade-union satire | Mohanlal plays a Gulf returnee who buys a bus and is crushed by union demands. Directed by Sathyan Anthikad and written by Sreenivasan. Still the classic film about doing business in Kerala. |
| **Ustad Hotel** (2012, Malayalam) | A Kozhikode restaurant | Dulquer Salmaan as a chef who comes back to his grandfather's café. It made *sulaimani* tea famous beyond Malabar. |
| **Manjummel Boys** (2024, Malayalam) | Tourism, the Guna Caves | Its success sent visitor numbers to Kodaikanal's Guna Caves soaring. It was the highest-grossing Malayalam film when it came out. |
