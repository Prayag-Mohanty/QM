Sixth in the Christ Com prep set: management practices with who invented them and the story behind the name, then the business and finance books a quizmaster is likely to ask about.

## Quiz-ready

If you only have ten minutes, revise these.

1. **Kanban** was inspired by American supermarket restocking. **Jidoka** came from Sakichi Toyoda's loom, which stopped itself when a thread broke.
2. **Poka-yoke** was first called *baka-yoke* ("fool-proofing"). Shigeo Shingo.
3. **Lean** was coined by John Krafcik (1988), later CEO of Waymo.
4. **Six Sigma**: Bill Smith at Motorola (1986). Its experts are ranked by karate belts.
5. **Deming** taught Japan quality; Japan named its top quality prize after him (1951).
6. **Pareto**: the peas in his garden. Joseph Juran named the 80/20 principle after him.
7. **BCG matrix**: stars, cash cows, question marks, dogs. Bruce Henderson, 1970.
8. **Porter's Five Forces**: HBR, 1979. His consultancy Monitor went bankrupt in 2012.
9. **Blue Ocean**: Cirque du Soleil. **Jobs to be done**: the milkshake. **Disruption**: Christensen.
10. **Maslow** never drew the pyramid. **Hawthorne**: the lighting experiments at Western Electric.
11. **OKRs**: Andy Grove at Intel, taken to Google by John Doerr in 1999.
12. **Johari window** = Joseph Luft + Harrington Ingham.
13. **Pomodoro** = a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. **Brainstorming** = Alex Osborn, the "O" of BBDO.
14. **Scrum** is named after rugby. The **Agile Manifesto** was written at a ski lodge in Snowbird, Utah (2001). **Skunk Works** comes from the *Li'l Abner* comic.
15. **Amazon**: no PowerPoint, six-page memos, two-pizza teams. **Netflix**: the culture deck and the keeper test. **3M**: the Post-it Note.
16. **Dabbawalas** (1890). **Amul**: Kurien from Kozhikode and Operation Flood. **Aravind** was inspired by McDonald's.
17. ***The Goal*** is a novel. ***Zero to One*** began as class notes. ***Good to Great***'s Circuit City later failed.
18. ***The Intelligent Investor***'s Mr. Market. ***A Random Walk***'s blindfolded monkey. ***Reminiscences*** is Jesse Livermore's life.
19. ***Cadbury's Purple Reign*** came up in Major Nair's prelims.
20. ***The Polyester Prince*** (Ambani) was kept out of Indian shops.

## Japanese and quality practices

Most of these came out of Toyota after the war, often by borrowing from the US. Kanban was inspired by American supermarket shelves, and the quality movement was taught to Japan by the American W. Edwards Deming.

| Practice | Meaning | Origin story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Toyota Production System (TPS)** | Toyota's whole way of making cars: build only what's needed, and stop to fix problems. | Built by **Taiichi Ohno** and Eiji Toyoda from the late 1940s. Its two pillars are just-in-time and *jidoka*. |
| **Kaizen** | Continuous small improvements by everyone. | Japanese for "change for the better". Masaaki Imai's book *Kaizen* (1986) spread it to the West. |
| **Kanban** | A card or signal that tells the previous step to make more. | Japanese for "signboard". Ohno was inspired by **American supermarkets**, which restock shelves only when customers take goods. |
| **Just-in-time (JIT)** | Making and receiving parts only as needed, with minimal stock. | Kiichiro Toyoda's idea, put into practice by Ohno. |
| **Jidoka** | Machines and workers stop automatically when something goes wrong. | Sakichi Toyoda's 1924 **automatic loom** stopped when a thread broke. Toyota itself started as Toyoda Automatic Loom Works. |
| **Andon cord** | A cord any worker can pull to stop the line. | *Andon* is a Japanese paper lantern. |
| **Poka-yoke** | Mistake-proofing, so a wrong action is impossible. | **Shigeo Shingo**. It was first called *baka-yoke* ("fool-proofing") and renamed because that was insulting. A USB plug that only fits one way is an everyday example. |
| **SMED** | Single-minute exchange of die: changing tooling in under 10 minutes. | Shingo again. |
| **5S** | *Seiri, seiton, seisō, seiketsu, shitsuke*: sort, set in order, shine, standardise, sustain. | A Japanese workplace-tidiness method. |
| **Muda, mura, muri** | Waste, unevenness, overburden. | The three enemies in Toyota's system. Ohno listed seven wastes, and "unused talent" is often added as an eighth. |
| **Gemba / genchi genbutsu** | "The real place" / "go and see for yourself". | Managers should walk the shop floor. |
| **Five Whys** | Ask "why?" five times to reach a root cause. | **Sakichi Toyoda**. |
| **Quality circles / fishbone diagram** | Small worker groups that solve problems / a cause-and-effect chart shaped like a fish skeleton. | **Kaoru Ishikawa**, which is why the fishbone is also called the Ishikawa diagram. |
| **PDCA / Deming cycle** | Plan, do, check, act. | Walter **Shewhart**'s idea, spread by **W. Edwards Deming**, who lectured in Japan in 1950. Japan named its top quality award the **Deming Prize** (1951). |
| **Lean** | The Western name for Toyota-style production. | Coined by MIT researcher **John Krafcik** in 1988. He later became CEO of Waymo. Popularised by *The Machine That Changed the World* (1990). |
| **Six Sigma** | Cutting defects to 3.4 per million opportunities. | **Bill Smith** at **Motorola**, 1986. **Jack Welch** made it famous at GE. Its experts are ranked by karate-style belts: Green, Black, Master Black. |
| **TQM** (Total Quality Management) | Quality as everyone's job. | Grew from Deming, Juran and Crosby. Philip Crosby coined "zero defects" and "quality is free". |
| **Pareto principle (80/20)** | About 80% of effects come from 20% of causes. | **Vilfredo Pareto** noticed 20% of Italians owned 80% of the land, and that 20% of the pea pods in his garden gave 80% of the peas. Quality expert **Joseph Juran** named it after him. |
| **Theory of Constraints** | A system's output is limited by its bottleneck. | **Eliyahu Goldratt**, who explained it in a **novel**, *The Goal* (1984). |
| **Taguchi methods** | Designing products that hold up under variation. | Genichi Taguchi. |

## Strategy frameworks

The strategy toolkit comes mostly from Harvard Business School and the consulting firms. BCG gave us the cash cow, McKinsey the 7S, and Michael Porter the five forces.

| Framework | What it does | Origin story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SWOT** | Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats. | Usually credited to **Albert Humphrey** at Stanford Research Institute in the 1960s. The credit is disputed: Harvard's Learned and Andrews were teaching similar ideas. |
| **PEST / PESTLE** | Political, economic, social, technological (+ legal, environmental) factors. | Francis Aguilar's "ETPS" (1967). |
| **Porter's Five Forces** | Rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power. | **Michael Porter**, HBR article of **1979**, then *Competitive Strategy* (1980). |
| **Generic strategies / value chain** | Cost leadership, differentiation or focus / the activities that add value. | Porter again (1980, 1985). Porter co-founded the consultancy Monitor, which went bankrupt in 2012, a classic quiz irony. |
| **BCG growth-share matrix** | **Stars, cash cows, question marks, dogs**. | **Bruce Henderson**, founder of Boston Consulting Group, 1970. BCG also popularised the **experience curve**. |
| **GE–McKinsey nine-box** | A 3×3 grid of industry attractiveness against business strength. | Built for GE in the 1970s. |
| **Ansoff matrix** | Market penetration, market development, product development, diversification. | **Igor Ansoff**, HBR article of 1957. |
| **McKinsey 7S** | Strategy, structure, systems, shared values, style, staff, skills. | Tom Peters, Robert Waterman and others (1980). It led to their bestseller *In Search of Excellence* (1982). |
| **Core competence** | Build the company around what it does uniquely well. | **C.K. Prahalad** and Gary Hamel, HBR (1990). |
| **Blue Ocean Strategy** | Create uncontested market space instead of fighting in a "red ocean" of rivals. | **W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne** of INSEAD (2005). Their star example is **Cirque du Soleil**. |
| **Disruptive innovation** | Cheap, "good enough" newcomers start at the bottom of the market and eventually topple leaders. | **Clayton Christensen**, *The Innovator's Dilemma* (1997). Andy Grove and Steve Jobs both cited it. |
| **Jobs to be done** | Customers "hire" products to do a job. | Christensen's **milkshake** study: commuters bought morning milkshakes to make a long drive less boring. |
| **Balanced Scorecard** | Measuring financial, customer, internal-process and learning goals. | **Robert Kaplan and David Norton**, HBR (1992). |
| **Flywheel / hedgehog concept / BHAG** | Self-reinforcing momentum / what you can be best at / a "big hairy audacious goal". | **Jim Collins** (*Good to Great*, *Built to Last*). Bezos sketched Amazon's flywheel on a napkin in 2001. |
| **Reverse innovation** | Products designed for emerging markets, then sold back to rich ones. | Vijay Govindarajan with GE's Jeff Immelt (2009), with GE's low-cost ECG machines from India as the example. |
| **Bottom of the pyramid** | Serving the world's poorest as profitable customers. | **C.K. Prahalad**, *The Fortune at the Bottom of the Pyramid* (2004). The **shampoo sachet** is the classic Indian example. |
| **Three horizons** | Balancing the current core, emerging businesses and future options. | McKinsey's *The Alchemy of Growth* (1999). |
| **Marketing mix (4 Ps)** | Product, price, place, promotion. | **E. Jerome McCarthy** (1960), building on Neil Borden. Philip Kotler made it famous. |

## People, productivity and project methods

Several famous ideas aren't quite what people think. Maslow never drew the pyramid, the Hawthorne findings are disputed, and the man who described the "waterfall" model was warning against it.

### Managing people

| Idea | Meaning | Origin story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scientific management (Taylorism)** | Break work into timed steps and pay by output. | **Frederick Winslow Taylor**, *The Principles of Scientific Management* (1911). His pig-iron loader at Bethlehem Steel, "Schmidt", became the famous example. |
| **Gantt chart** | Bars on a timeline showing tasks. | **Henry Gantt**, a colleague of Taylor's, in the 1910s. It was used on the Hoover Dam. |
| **Fayol's 14 principles** | Division of work, unity of command and others. | Henri Fayol, French mining engineer (1916). |
| **Bureaucracy** | Rules, hierarchy and merit. | Sociologist **Max Weber**. |
| **Hawthorne effect** | People change their behaviour when they know they are being watched. | Experiments at Western Electric's Hawthorne Works, Chicago (1924–32), linked to **Elton Mayo**. Output rose whether the lights went up or down. Later re-analyses doubt the findings. |
| **Maslow's hierarchy of needs** | Physiological, safety, love and belonging, esteem, self-actualisation. | **Abraham Maslow** (1943). He never drew the **pyramid**; consultants added it decades later. |
| **Herzberg's two-factor theory** | "Hygiene" factors prevent unhappiness; motivators create satisfaction. | Frederick Herzberg (1959). |
| **Theory X / Theory Y / Theory Z** | Workers are lazy / workers are self-motivated / lifetime employment and group decisions, Japanese-style. | Douglas McGregor, *The Human Side of Enterprise* (1960). William Ouchi added Theory Z (1981). |
| **Management by Objectives (MBO)** | Manager and employee agree on goals together. | **Peter Drucker**, *The Practice of Management* (1954). |
| **OKRs** | Objectives and key results. | **Andy Grove** at Intel. **John Doerr** learned it there and took it to **Google** in 1999. His book: *Measure What Matters* (2018). |
| **SMART goals** | Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound. | George Doran (1981). |
| **Tuckman's stages** | Forming, storming, norming, performing (and adjourning). | Bruce Tuckman (1965; "adjourning" added in 1977). |
| **Johari window** | What you know and others know about you, in four panes. | Joseph **Luft** and **Har**rington **In**gham (1955). The name is "Jo" + "Hari". |
| **Peter principle** | People are promoted until they reach their level of incompetence. | Laurence J. Peter (1969). |
| **Parkinson's law** | Work expands to fill the time available. | C. Northcote Parkinson, in an essay in *The Economist* (1955). |
| **Situational leadership / The One Minute Manager** | Adjust your style to the person's readiness. | Paul Hersey and **Ken Blanchard**. |
| **Servant leadership** | The leader serves the team. | Robert Greenleaf (1970), inspired by Hermann Hesse's *Journey to the East*. |
| **Vitality curve ("rank and yank")** | Rank staff 20-70-10 and remove the bottom 10%. | **Jack Welch** at GE. |
| **Management by walking around** | Leaders wander the floor and talk to people. | Part of **"the HP Way"**, Hewlett-Packard's culture. |
| **Change models** | Unfreeze, change, refreeze / Kotter's 8 steps / ADKAR. | Kurt Lewin (1940s) / John Kotter (1995) / Prosci. |
| **Cultural dimensions** | Power distance, individualism and other measures of national culture. | Geert **Hofstede**, from a survey of IBM employees worldwide. |

### Productivity and creativity

| Method | Meaning | Origin story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Brainstorming** | No-criticism idea generation in a group. | **Alex Osborn**, the "O" of the ad agency BBDO (1940s–53). |
| **Six Thinking Hats** | Six coloured "hats" for different modes of thinking. | **Edward de Bono** (1985), who also coined "lateral thinking". |
| **Mind map** | A radial diagram of ideas. | Tony Buzan (1970s). |
| **Eisenhower matrix** | Sort tasks by urgent versus important. | From Eisenhower's line about urgent and important problems. Stephen Covey popularised the grid. |
| **Pomodoro technique** | 25 minutes of work, then a short break. | **Francesco Cirillo**, as a student in the late 1980s. He used a **tomato-shaped kitchen timer**, and *pomodoro* is Italian for tomato. |
| **Getting Things Done (GTD)** | Capture everything in a trusted system. | David Allen (2001). |
| **Delphi method** | Anonymous rounds of expert forecasts until they agree. | **RAND Corporation**, 1950s, named after the Oracle of Delphi. |
| **Pyramid principle / MECE** | Lead with the answer; group ideas so they are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive. | **Barbara Minto**, McKinsey's first woman consultant (1960s–70s). |
| **RACI** | Responsible, accountable, consulted, informed. | — |
| **Net Promoter Score** | "How likely are you to recommend us?" on a 0–10 scale. | **Fred Reichheld** of Bain, HBR (2003). |

### Projects and software

| Method | Meaning | Origin story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PERT / CPM** | Network charts that find a project's critical path. | **PERT**: the US Navy's **Polaris** missile programme (1958). **CPM**: DuPont (1957). |
| **Waterfall** | Sequential phases: requirements, design, build, test. | Usually traced to **Winston Royce** (1970), whose paper actually argued **against** the pure version. |
| **Agile Manifesto** | Individuals, working software, customer collaboration and responding to change. | **17 developers** at a ski lodge in **Snowbird, Utah**, in February **2001**. |
| **Scrum** | Short sprints, with a daily stand-up. | Named after the **rugby** scrum in **Takeuchi and Nonaka**'s 1986 HBR article. Turned into a software method by Jeff Sutherland and Ken Schwaber. |
| **Brooks's law** | Adding people to a late software project makes it later. | Fred Brooks, *The Mythical Man-Month* (1975). |
| **Skunk Works** | A small, secret, autonomous team. | **Lockheed**'s 1943 jet-fighter team under Kelly Johnson. It's named after the moonshine "Skonk Works" in the comic strip *Li'l Abner*. |
| **Design thinking** | Empathise, define, ideate, prototype, test. | Popularised by **IDEO** and Stanford's d.school. |
| **Lean Startup / MVP** | Build-measure-learn with a minimum viable product. | **Eric Ries** (2011), drawing on Steve Blank. |

## Company rituals and Indian models

Quizmasters love a company's quirky rule (Amazon's ban on PowerPoint, 3M's 15% time) and India's frugal models (dabbawalas, Amul, Aravind). Amul has a Kerala link: Verghese Kurien was born in Kozhikode.

### Famous company practices

| Practice | Company | Story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Six-page memo** | Amazon | Bezos banned PowerPoint in senior meetings in **2004**. Meetings start with everyone silently reading a narrative memo. |
| **Working backwards (PR/FAQ)** | Amazon | Write the launch press release before building the product. |
| **Two-pizza team** | Amazon | No team should be bigger than two pizzas can feed. |
| **"Day 1"** | Amazon | Bezos's shareholder letters keep the company thinking like a start-up. "Day 2 is stasis, followed by irrelevance". Amazon named an office building Day 1. Also: "disagree and commit", and the door-desk award for frugality. |
| **Culture deck** | Netflix | Reed Hastings and Patty McCord's 2009 slide deck: "freedom and responsibility", and the **keeper test** (would you fight to keep this person?). Sheryl Sandberg called it perhaps the most important document to come out of the Valley. The book is *No Rules Rules* (2020). |
| **20% time** | Google | Engineers could spend a fifth of their time on side projects. **Gmail** and AdSense are credited to it. "Don't be evil" was moved from the front of its code of conduct in 2018. |
| **15% rule** | 3M | Since 1948. The **Post-it Note** came from Spencer Silver's "failed" weak glue and **Art Fry**'s need for a bookmark that stayed in his hymn book. |
| **Psychological safety** | Google (Project Aristotle) | Google's study of its teams found the best ones were those where people felt safe to take risks. |
| **Pay to quit** | Zappos, Amazon | New hires were offered money to leave if they didn't fit. Zappos also tried **holacracy**, a system with no managers. |
| **Squads and tribes** | Spotify | Small autonomous teams (2012), widely copied as "the Spotify model". |
| **Lattice organisation** | W.L. Gore (makers of Gore-Tex) | No job titles, and plants capped at about 150 people. |
| **Industrial democracy** | Semco, Brazil | Ricardo Semler's book *Maverick*: workers set their own pay and hours. |
| **Constructive confrontation** | Intel | Andy Grove's culture of open argument. |

### Indian models

| Model | Origin | Quiz hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mumbai dabbawalas** | Started in 1890 by Mahadeo Havaji Bachche; about 5,000 carriers deliver lunchboxes by train. | A coded marking system works without computers. The often-quoted "Six Sigma" rating or "one error in 16 million" comes from media reports, not a formal certification. Prince Charles and Richard Branson have visited them. |
| **Amul / Anand pattern** | 1946, Kaira District Cooperative, founded by **Tribhuvandas Patel** with **Verghese Kurien**. | Kurien, born in **Kozhikode**, led **Operation Flood** (1970), the world's largest dairy development programme, and is called the "Milkman of India". His autobiography is *I Too Had a Dream*. **Amul** stands for Anand Milk Union Limited, and *amulya* means "priceless". |
| **Aravind Eye Care** | 1976, Madurai, Dr G. Venkataswamy ("Dr V"). | Assembly-line cataract surgery, inspired by **McDonald's**. Its Aurolab makes cheap intraocular lenses. Paying patients subsidise free ones. |
| **Narayana Health** | Dr Devi Shetty, Bengaluru. | Called the "Walmart-isation" of heart surgery. |
| **Jaipur Foot** | 1969, sculptor-craftsman Ram Chandra Sharma and Dr P.K. Sethi. | A low-cost prosthetic limb used worldwide. |
| **Lijjat Papad** | 1959, seven Gujarati women with a ₹80 loan. | Its members are called "sisters", and the mascot is a rabbit. |
| **ITC e-Choupal** | 2000: internet kiosks for farmers. | A famous Harvard case study. *Choupal* means village meeting place. |
| **HUL Project Shakti** | 2001: rural women as distributors. | A bottom-of-the-pyramid example. |
| **Kudumbashree** | 1998, Kerala: a network of women's self-help groups. | Kerala's anti-poverty mission. |
| **Jugaad innovation** | Frugal improvisation. | Navi Radjou, Jaideep Prabhu and Simone Ahuja, *Jugaad Innovation* (2012). |

## Finance, investing and Indian business books

Warren Buffett's favourite, *The Intelligent Investor*, is the most-asked finance book. *The Richest Man in Babylon* turns 100 in 2026. On the Indian side, the Kerala hooks are Verghese Kurien's memoir and the authorised Ratan Tata biography by Kerala-born Thomas Mathew.
