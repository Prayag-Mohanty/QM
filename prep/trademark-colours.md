## Quiz-ready: likely questions

1. **Which 1995 US Supreme Court case said a colour alone can be a trademark?** *Qualitex v. Jacobson*, over green-gold press pads.
2. **Whose nail polish gave Louboutin its red sole?** An assistant's, painted on a prototype in 1993.
3. **Which number is Tiffany Blue, and why?** Pantone 1837, the year Tiffany was founded.
4. **Which company stopped Cadbury from owning Pantone 2685C purple?** Nestlé (Court of Appeal, 2013).
5. **What is UPS's brown named after?** Pullman railway carriages: "Pullman Brown".
6. **Which brand used the Pink Panther, and why does it matter?** Owens Corning. Its pink insulation was the first single-colour trademark in the US (1985).
7. **Why are Post-it notes yellow?** The lab next door only had yellow scrap paper.
8. **Why is Facebook blue?** Zuckerberg is red–green colour-blind.
9. **Which newspapers gave cycling's yellow and pink jerseys their colours?** *L'Auto* (Tour de France) and *La Gazzetta dello Sport* (Giro d'Italia).
10. **Why is Ferrari's badge yellow?** It is the colour of Modena. The horse came from WWI pilot Francesco Baracca.
11. **Which home-improvement chain lost its abstract orange trademark in Germany's top court on 17 Sep 2026?** OBI, after challenges from Hornbach and Globus.
12. **Which battery maker won a red trade-dress injunction against Amara Raja in April 2026?** Exide (Calcutta HC).
13. **Which artist holds the exclusive art licence to Vantablack, and which paint was sold to everyone but him?** Anish Kapoor; Stuart Semple's "Pinkest Pink".
14. **What is Pantone's Colour of the Year for 2026, and why is it unusual?** Cloud Dancer (11-4201), the first white.
15. **Which colour is called the "world's ugliest" and is used on plain cigarette packs?** Pantone 448C, Opaque Couché.
16. **What was 18-year-old William Perkin trying to make when he discovered mauve?** Quinine.
17. **Which company is the only maker of India's election ink?** Mysore Paints & Varnish Ltd.
18. **In Kathakali, which make-up type marks noble heroes like Arjuna?** Pacha (green).
19. **Why did Pepsi paint a Concorde blue?** For Project Blue (1996), its rebrand to blue.
20. **Why was the FT printed on pink paper?** Unbleached paper was cheaper, and the colour made it stand out (1893).

## Colour trademarks and landmark cases

**The rule in one line:** a single colour can be a trademark only if consumers see it as pointing to one source ("secondary meaning"), and it can't be **functional** (orange for safety, black for outboard motors).

| Colour / owner | What is protected | Case and story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Green-gold, Qualitex** | Dry-cleaning press pads | ***Qualitex v. Jacobson*** **(US Supreme Court, 1995)**: a colour alone can be a trademark. The founding case.\* |
| **Pink, Owens Corning** | Fibreglass insulation | ***In re Owens-Corning*** **(1985)**: the **first single-colour trademark** in the US. Its mascot is the **Pink Panther**.\* |
| **Red sole, Christian Louboutin** | Lacquered red sole, Pantone 18-1663 TPX | In 1993 an assistant's **red nail polish** was painted on a prototype sole. ***Louboutin v. YSL*** **(2nd Cir., 2012)**: protected only when the red sole **contrasts** with the upper. The **CJEU (2018, *Van Haren*)** upheld it as a position mark. **Delhi High Court** first dismissed it with "a single colour can't be a mark"; the Division Bench reversed that, and Louboutin won in India.\* |
| **Tiffany Blue, Tiffany & Co.** | Box and colour (registered 1998) | Custom **Pantone 1837**, the founding year. Robin's-egg blue was a Victorian favourite. You can't buy the box; it comes only with a purchase.\* |
| **Purple, Cadbury** | Pantone **2685C** for milk chocolate | Used since 1914, and said to honour **Queen Victoria**. Applied for in 2004, opposed by **Nestlé**. The **Court of Appeal ruled for Nestlé in 2013**, saying the description was too vague, and a second attempt failed in 2022. So **Cadbury doesn't own purple** in the UK. |
| **Brown, UPS** | "Pullman Brown" trucks and uniforms | Named after **Pullman railway carriages**; also hides dirt. Slogan "**What can Brown do for you?**"\* |
| **Magenta, T-Mobile / Deutsche Telekom** | RAL 4010 (Telemagenta) | Notorious for suing over magenta. In 2019 it took on insurance start-up **Lemonade**'s pink.\* |
| **Green and yellow, John Deere** | Tractors | "Nothing runs like a Deere". Won colour cases against rivals copying the combination.\* |
| **Canary yellow, 3M** | **Post-it** notes | Chosen by accident: the **scrap paper next door was yellow**. Spencer Silver's "failed" weak glue (1968), plus Art Fry's need for a hymn-book bookmark.\* |
| **Barbie Pink, Mattel** | Pantone **219C** | The *Barbie* film (2023) caused a global shortage of **Rosco** fluorescent pink paint.\* |
| **Lilac, Milka (Mondelez)** | Wrapper and the lilac cow | Registered in Germany and the EU.\* |
| **Gold, Lindt** | Gold-foil **Gold Bunny** | Lost the shape case at the CJEU (2012), but **won on the gold colour in Germany's BGH (2021)**.\* |
| **Yellow-orange, Veuve Clicquot** | Champagne label | Registered colour mark.\* |
| **Red, Sparkasse** | German savings banks, HKS 13 | Beat **Santander**'s red in the German courts (2016).\* |
| **Yellow, Langenscheidt** | Dictionaries | Beat **Rosetta Stone** in the German BGH (2014).\* |
| **Orange, OBI** | DIY chain, RAL 2008 | **New, 17 Sep 2026:** Germany's **BGH** (I ZB 58/25) **cancelled** OBI's abstract orange mark after challenges from **Hornbach and Globus**. Surveys didn't show that consumers link orange only to OBI, and other DIY chains use it. OBI can still use orange. |
| **Red, Exide (India)** | Red battery trade dress | **New, 2 Apr 2026:** **Calcutta High Court** upheld an injunction for **Exide** against **Amara Raja** (Amaron). Protection is for its long-used red-and-artwork combination, not red itself. |
| **Red and white, Colgate (India)** | Toothpaste packaging | ***Colgate v. Anchor*** **(Delhi HC, 2003)**: colour combinations are protectable under passing off. |
| **Gold foil, Ferrero Rocher (India)** | Wrapper and trade dress | Delhi HC permanent injunction against "**Golden Passion**" chocolates. |

*\* From general knowledge; no source was checked in this session.*

## Signature colours by industry

### Luxury and fashion

- **Hermès orange**: the orange box was a WWII shortage accident, when the cream card ran out.
- **Valentino red**: inspired by costumes at a Barcelona opera.
- **Bottega Veneta "Parakeet" green**: made famous under Daniel Lee (2021) and kept by his successors.
- **Cartier red box** and **Tiffany Blue box**.
- **Louboutin red sole**.
- **Burberry Nova check**: camel, black, red and white.
- **Klein Blue**: see the next section.

### Food and drink

- **Coca-Cola red**: barrels were painted red so tax agents could tell alcohol from Coke. The myth that Coke **invented red Santa** is false: Haddon Sundblom's 1931 ads only popularised him.
- **Pepsi blue**: on **Project Blue** (1996) Pepsi painted an **Air France Concorde** blue.
- **McDonald's red and yellow**: the "ketchup and mustard" theory. **Green** is used in Europe for eco messaging.
- **Starbucks green**: changed from brown in 1987, when Il Giornale merged with Starbucks.
- **Cadbury purple**, **Milka lilac**, **KitKat red**, **Veuve Clicquot yellow**.
- **Heinz** turquoise-keystone label.
- **Kingfisher red**.

### Tech and media

- **Facebook blue**: Zuckerberg is **red–green colour-blind**, and blue is the colour he sees best.
- **IBM is "Big Blue"**: the logo is Paul Rand's 8-bar blue, and Deep Blue beat Kasparov.
- **Netflix red**.
- **Spotify green**.
- **Twitter blue** went to **X black** in 2023.
- **Google**: the primary colours with one **secondary (green "l")**, to show it doesn't follow the rules.
- **Apple's rainbow logo** (1977–98, by Rob Janoff): six stripes, with green at the top.
- **LinkedIn, Twitter (once), Samsung, Intel and PayPal all use blue, the most common tech colour because it signals trust**.
- **Financial Times salmon pink** (1893): unbleached paper was cheaper, and the colour stood out from the *Financial News*.

### Telecom

- **T-Mobile magenta**, **Vodafone red**.
- **Orange**: the brand is literally a colour, and it won ads with "The future's bright".
- **O2 blue bubbles**.
- **Airtel red** and **Jio blue**: see India.

### Logistics and retail

- **UPS brown**.
- **DHL yellow and red**: from Deutsche Post's posthorn yellow.
- **FedEx purple and orange**: the **hidden arrow** between the E and the x.
- **IKEA blue and yellow**: the **Swedish flag**.
- **Target red**: Bullseye the dog.
- **Home Depot orange**.
- **Hertz yellow**: John Hertz founded the **Yellow Cab Company** (1915) after a University of Chicago study suggested yellow was the easiest colour to spot.
- **Caterpillar yellow**.

### Airlines

- **easyJet orange** (Pantone 021C): Stelios chose it because it was cheap and stood out.
- **IndiGo indigo**.
- **Air India's red, aubergine and gold**: from its **2023 rebrand, "The Vista"**, by FutureBrand, a window-frame shape.
- **Qantas red**: the kangaroo.
- **KLM blue**.
- **Southwest heart**.
- **Emirates red**: the Arabic calligraphy logo.

### Sport and cars

- **Ferrari Rosso Corsa**: **Italy's national racing colour**. The **yellow** in its badge is the colour of **Modena**, and the prancing horse came from WWI ace **Francesco Baracca**.
- **British Racing Green**.
- **French blue**.
- **German white**, which became the **Silver Arrows**: legend says Mercedes **scraped off the white paint** in 1934 to make weight.
- **Tour de France yellow jersey**: the colour of **L'Auto**'s newsprint.
- **Giro d'Italia pink jersey** (maglia rosa): the colour of **La Gazzetta dello Sport**'s paper.
- **Wimbledon purple and green**.
- **The Masters green jacket**.
- **Team India's "Men in Blue"**: from 1992 World Cup coloured kits.

## Pantone, named colours and colour-business trivia

- **Pantone**: **Lawrence Herbert** joined the printer M&J Levine and bought it in 1962. The **Pantone Matching System** (1963) numbers colours so that printers everywhere match. Pantone is now owned by **X-Rite**, part of **Veralto** (spun off from Danaher).\*
- **Colour of the Year** (since 2000, when it was **Cerulean**): 2024 Peach Fuzz, 2025 **Mocha Mousse**, and **2026 Cloud Dancer (PANTONE 11-4201)**, an off-white and the **first white ever chosen**.
- **Pantone 448C, "Opaque Couché"**: voted the **world's ugliest colour** and used on **plain cigarette packs** in Australia (2012), then the UK and others.\*
- **International Klein Blue (IKB)**: artist **Yves Klein** registered the formula through a French **Soleau envelope** in 1960. This is a record, not a true patent or trademark.\*
- **Vantablack**: made by Surrey NanoSystems; it absorbs about 99.96% of light. Artist **Anish Kapoor** has the **exclusive art licence** (2016). In protest, Stuart Semple released "**the Pinkest Pink**", which anyone could buy **except Anish Kapoor**.\*
- **Mauve**: **William Perkin**, aged 18, made **mauveine** (1856), the first synthetic dye, by accident while trying to make **quinine**. It started the chemical industry.\*
- **Prussian blue** (1706): the first modern synthetic pigment, and the blue in blueprints and Hokusai's *Great Wave*.\*
- **Indian Yellow**: a pigment said to come from the **urine of cows fed mango leaves**, from Monghyr, Bihar. The story is disputed.\*
- **Tyrian purple**: made from murex snails, which is why purple became the colour of royalty.\*
- **Brat green** (2024): Charli XCX's album. Collins named "brat" its Word of the Year.\*
- **Marrs Green**: chosen by the public in a University of Strathclyde/Scottish Water poll as the "world's favourite colour" (2017).\*
- **Mountbatten pink**: a naval camouflage colour. **Millennial pink** and **Barbiecore**.\*

| **Jio blue** | Many shades of blue across the Reliance consumer brands.\* |

### Kerala colour hooks

- **Kasavu**: off-white with **gold zari**, the Kerala sari and mundu for Onam and Vishu. **Balaramapuram** and **Kuthampully** (Thrissur district) are GI-tagged.
- **Kathakali make-up (vesham)** colours tell you the character:
  - **Pacha** (green): noble heroes such as Krishna and Arjuna.
  - **Kathi** (knife): green with red marks, for arrogant villains such as Ravana.
  - **Thaadi** (beard): red for evil, white for divine (Hanuman), black for hunters.
  - **Kari** (black): demonesses.
  - **Minukku** (glowing yellow-orange): women and sages.\*
- **Thrissur Pooram's Kudamattam**: a rapid exchange of **colourful silk parasols** on elephants between the **Thiruvambady** and **Paramekkavu** groups at Vadakkunnathan temple.\*
- **Theyyam** (North Malabar): red-dominant face painting.\*
- **Kerala red**: the CPI(M) and the world's **first elected communist government** (EMS, 1957).\*
- **Gold:** **Malabar Gold**, **Joyalukkas** and **Kalyan**: the jewellers' gold standard. Thrissur is called the gold capital of Kerala.\*
