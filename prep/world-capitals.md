A companion to [World Flags and Emblems](../world-flags-and-emblems/). It lists the capital of every **UN member state** plus **Vatican City, Palestine, Kosovo and Taiwan**, grouped by continent, with the **origin and meaning of the name**, a **short history** and the **facts quizzes ask about**. Facts marked \* are traditional or disputed explanations.

## Records and oddities

- **Highest capital**: **La Paz**, Bolivia's seat of government, at about **3,640 m**. **Quito** (about 2,850 m) is the highest capital that is the sole official capital; Bolivia's constitutional capital **Sucre** is about 2,800 m.
- **Lowest capital**: **Baku**, about **28 m below sea level**, the only national capital below sea level.
- **Northernmost**: **Reykjavík** (64°N). **Southernmost**: **Wellington** (41°S).
- **Biggest**: **Tokyo**'s metro area (about **37 million**). **Smallest**: **Vatican City**; among ordinary towns, **Ngerulmud** (Palau) has only a few hundred people.
- **Oldest**: **Damascus** is often called the oldest continuously inhabited capital; **Athens**, **Jerusalem**, **Beirut** and **Lisbon** are all about 3,000 years old or more.
- **Closest capitals**: **Rome** surrounds **Vatican City**. Apart from that pair, **Kinshasa** and **Brazzaville**, facing each other across the **Congo River** (about 1.6 km at the narrowest), are the closest. In Europe, **Vienna** and **Bratislava** are about **55 km** apart.
- **Easy to mix up**: **Kingston** (Jamaica) and **Kingstown** (St Vincent); **Georgetown** (Guyana) and **George Town** (Cayman Islands, a British territory); **Brazzaville** and **Kinshasa**; **Niamey** (Niger) and **Abuja** (Nigeria); **Bratislava** and **Ljubljana**; **Austria/Australia** (Vienna/Canberra).
- **Capital is not the largest city**: **Canberra** (Sydney), **Ottawa** (Toronto), **Washington DC** (New York), **Brasília** (São Paulo), **Abuja** (Lagos), **Ankara** (Istanbul), **Islamabad** (Karachi), **Wellington** (Auckland), **Bern** (Zürich), **Dodoma** (Dar es Salaam), **Yamoussoukro** (Abidjan), **Naypyidaw** (Yangon), **Astana** (Almaty), **Rabat** (Casablanca).
- **More than one capital**: **South Africa** (Pretoria, Cape Town, Bloemfontein), **Bolivia** (Sucre and La Paz), **Netherlands** (Amsterdam is the capital; **The Hague** is the seat of government), **Malaysia** (Kuala Lumpur, with the government in **Putrajaya**), **Sri Lanka** (Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte, with Colombo the commercial capital), **Benin** (Porto-Novo, with the government in Cotonou), **Côte d'Ivoire** (Yamoussoukro, with most offices in Abidjan), **Eswatini** (Mbabane, with the royal and legislative capital at Lobamba).
- **No official capital**: **Nauru**; the government sits in **Yaren** district. **Switzerland** has no legal capital: **Bern** is officially the *Bundesstadt* ("federal city").
- **Named after people**: **Washington** (George Washington), **Monrovia** (US President James Monroe), **Wellington** (the Duke of Wellington), **Georgetown** (King George III), **Victoria** (Queen Victoria), **Port Louis** (King Louis XV), **Castries** (the Marquis de Castries), **Brazzaville** (explorer Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza), **Lusaka** (a village chief, Lusaaka), **Kingston** and **Kingstown** (the English king), and many saints: **Santo Domingo**, **San José**, **San Salvador**, **São Tomé**, **Saint John's**, **Saint George's**, **Valletta** (Grand Master Jean de Valette).
- **Planned capitals**: **Washington DC** (1790s), **Canberra** (1913), **New Delhi** (1911–31), **Brasília** (1960), **Islamabad** (1960s), **Belmopan** (1970), **Gaborone** (1965), **Abuja** (1991), **Yamoussoukro** (1983), **Astana** (1997), **Putrajaya** (1999), **Naypyidaw** (2005), **Ngerulmud** (2006). **Egypt** is moving to the **New Administrative Capital** and **Indonesia** to **Nusantara** on Borneo.
- **Famous designers**: **Canberra** (Walter Burley Griffin and **Marion Mahony Griffin** of Chicago, who won a world competition), **Brasília** (Lúcio Costa's plan, shaped like an **aeroplane** or a bird, and Oscar Niemeyer's buildings), **New Delhi** (Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker), **Washington DC** (Pierre Charles L'Enfant), **Islamabad** (Constantinos Doxiadis), **Abuja** (Kenzo Tange's city centre).

## Asia

| Capital | Country | Name: origin and meaning | History and trivia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kabul** | Afghanistan | An ancient name, possibly the **Kubha** river of the **Rigveda** and Ptolemy's **Kabura**.\* | Became the capital in **1776** under **Timur Shah Durrani** (moved from Kandahar). **Babur**, founder of the Mughal empire, loved it and is buried in the **Bagh-e Babur** gardens. Sits at about **1,790 m** in a valley of the Hindu Kush. |
| **Yerevan** | Armenia | From **Erebuni**, the Urartian fortress founded by King **Argishti I** in **782 BC**, so the city celebrated its **2,750th** birthday in 2018. | One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities. Called the **"Pink City"** for its rosy **tuff** stone. **Mount Ararat**, now in Türkiye, dominates the skyline and appears on Armenia's coat of arms. |
| **Baku** | Azerbaijan | Often explained as Persian **Bad-kube**, "**wind-pounded city**"; Baku is famously windy.\* | The **world's lowest capital** (about 28 m below sea level) on the **Caspian Sea**. The world's first industrial oil boom (1870s): the **Nobel brothers** and the Rothschilds made fortunes here. The **Old City** with the **Maiden Tower** is a UNESCO site; the **Flame Towers** light up at night. Hosted **Eurovision 2012** and the **COP29** climate summit (2024). |
| **Manama** | Bahrain | Arabic, "**place of rest**" or "place of dreams".\* | On the northern tip of Bahrain island. Home of the **Bahrain World Trade Center** with wind turbines built between its towers. The **Bahrain Grand Prix** (Sakhir) was the **first F1 race in the Middle East** (2004). |
| **Dhaka** | Bangladesh | Possibly from the **dhak** tree (flame of the forest) or the goddess **Dhakeshwari**, whose temple is the national temple.\* | Mughal capital of Bengal from **1608/1610** (named **Jahangirnagar**). Famous for **muslin** so fine it was called "woven air". Known as the **"rickshaw capital of the world"** and the **"city of mosques"**. One of the most densely populated cities on Earth. |
| **Thimphu** | Bhutan | Local tradition explains it as "**dissolved/flat rock**" or the site of a temple built on a rock.\* | Capital only since **1961** (before that, **Punakha**). One of the very few capitals with **no traffic lights**: a policeman directs traffic in a pavilion at the main junction. The giant **Buddha Dordenma** statue overlooks the city. |
| **Bandar Seri Begawan** | Brunei | Malay, "**Port of the Seri Begawan**", the title taken by **Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III** after he abdicated (1967). Previously **Brunei Town**. | **Kampong Ayer**, the "**Venice of the East**", is the world's largest water village, built on stilts. The **Istana Nurul Iman** is the world's largest residential palace. |
| **Phnom Penh** | Cambodia | "**Penh's hill**": legend says a widow, **Daun Penh**, found four Buddha statues floating in the river (1372) and built a shrine on a hill, today's **Wat Phnom**. | Capital since **1434** (after Angkor was abandoned) and again from 1865. On the confluence of the **Mekong** and **Tonlé Sap** rivers. The **Khmer Rouge** emptied the city in **1975**; **Tuol Sleng (S-21)** is now a genocide museum. Once called the "**Pearl of Asia**". |
| **Beijing** | China | "**Northern capital**" (*bei* north + *jing* capital), as against **Nanjing** ("southern capital"). Older names include **Dadu/Khanbaliq** (Kublai Khan) and **Peiping** ("northern peace", 1928–49). | Capital for most of the last **800 years**. Home of the **Forbidden City** (the world's largest palace complex), **Tiananmen Square** and the **Temple of Heaven**. The **first city to host both the Summer (2008) and Winter (2022) Olympics**. All of China runs on **Beijing time** (one time zone). |
| **Tbilisi** | Georgia | From Old Georgian ***tbili***, "**warm**", after its **sulphur hot springs**. Legend: King **Vakhtang I Gorgasali** found them when his falcon fell into a spring while chasing a pheasant (5th century). | Formerly known abroad as **Tiflis**. Its **Abanotubani** bathhouses were praised by **Pushkin**. **Stalin** began his revolutionary career here. |
| **New Delhi** | India | "Delhi" is often traced to **Raja Dhilu** or Hindustani ***dehleez*** ("threshold", the gateway to the Gangetic plain).\* | Delhi has been the seat of many empires (said to be **"seven cities"** before New Delhi). **King George V** announced the move from Calcutta at the **Delhi Durbar** of **12 December 1911**; designed by **Edwin Lutyens** and **Herbert Baker**, inaugurated **13 February 1931**. Lutyens' **Rashtrapati Bhavan** was the Viceroy's House. Delhi has **three UNESCO sites**: Red Fort, Qutub Minar and Humayun's Tomb. |
| **Jakarta** | Indonesia | From Sanskrit ***Jayakarta***, "**victorious deed**" or "complete victory", the name given by **Fatahillah** after defeating the Portuguese (**22 June 1527**, the city's birthday). The Dutch called it **Batavia** (1619–1942). | The **Dutch East India Company (VOC)** headquarters in Asia. Sinking fast (parts of north Jakarta by up to **25 cm a year**), one reason the capital is moving to **Nusantara** ("archipelago") in **East Kalimantan, Borneo**. |
| **Tehran** | Iran | Possibly "**warm place**" or "lower place" (*tah* + *ran*), as opposed to the cooler **Shemiran** uphill.\* | A village until **Agha Mohammad Khan** of the **Qajar** dynasty made it capital in **1786**. Hosted the **Tehran Conference** (1943) of **Churchill, Roosevelt and Stalin**. Landmarks: the **Azadi Tower** (1971) and **Milad Tower**. Sits below the **Alborz** mountains and **Mount Damavand**. |
| **Baghdad** | Iraq | Usually derived from Middle Persian ***Bag-dad***, "**given by God**". Its founder, **Caliph al-Mansur**, called it **Madinat al-Salam**, "**City of Peace**" (762). | Built as a perfectly **round city**. Under the **Abbasids** it was the world's largest city and home of the **House of Wisdom** (*Bayt al-Hikma*). Setting of ***One Thousand and One Nights*** (Harun al-Rashid). Sacked by the **Mongols under Hulagu** in **1258**. |
| **Jerusalem** (proclaimed) | Israel | Often explained as "**foundation of (the god) Shalem**"; popularly linked to ***shalom***, "peace". | Holy to **Judaism, Christianity and Islam**: the **Western Wall**, the **Church of the Holy Sepulchre** and the **Al-Aqsa Mosque/Dome of the Rock**. Israel calls it its capital; most countries keep embassies in **Tel Aviv**. The **US** moved its embassy here in **2018**. The **Old City** is divided into four quarters. |
| **Tokyo** | Japan | "**Eastern capital**" (*tō* east + *kyō* capital), named in **1868**; before that it was **Edo** ("estuary"). | **Tokugawa Ieyasu** made Edo the shogun's seat in **1603**. Emperor **Meiji** moved from **Kyoto** in 1868–69. Rebuilt after the **1923 Great Kantō earthquake** and the **1945 firebombing**. The world's **largest metropolitan area**. Hosted the **1964** and **2020 (held 2021) Olympics**. **Shibuya Crossing** is often called the world's busiest. |
| **Amman** | Jordan | From the **Ammonites**, whose capital was **Rabbath Ammon**. The Greeks renamed it **Philadelphia** after **Ptolemy II Philadelphus**. | Built on **seven hills** (*jabals*), now many more. A **Roman theatre** seating 6,000 sits in the city centre. A small town until it became capital of **Transjordan** in **1921**. |
| **Astana** | Kazakhstan | Kazakh, simply "**capital**". Earlier names: **Akmolinsk**, **Tselinograd** ("Virgin Lands city"), **Akmola** ("white grave" or "white shrine"), and **Nur-Sultan** (2019–22, after ex-president Nazarbayev). | Replaced **Almaty** in **1997**. A showpiece of futuristic buildings: the **Bayterek** tower (a poplar holding a golden egg), **Khan Shatyr** (a giant transparent tent by **Norman Foster**) and the **Palace of Peace and Reconciliation** pyramid. Among the coldest capitals, with winter lows below **−40 °C**. |
| **Kuwait City** | Kuwait | **Kuwait** is a diminutive of Arabic ***kut***, "**fortress**" near water: "little fort". | Founded in the early 18th century by the **Bani Utbah**; the **Al-Sabah** family has ruled since 1752. Invaded by **Iraq** on **2 August 1990**, leading to the **Gulf War**. The **Kuwait Towers** (1979) are the national symbol. |
| **Bishkek** | Kyrgyzstan | From a word for the **paddle used to churn kumis** (fermented mare's milk), or from a hero named **Bishkek**.\* Called **Pishpek**, then **Frunze** (1926–91) after Bolshevik commander **Mikhail Frunze**, who was born here. | Began as a **Kokand khanate** fort (1825). Laid out in a Soviet **grid** with many trees, one of the greenest Central Asian cities. Sits under the snowy **Ala-Too** range. |
| **Vientiane** | Laos | From Pali/Sanskrit, "**city of sandalwood**" (*Viang Chan*). The French spelling stuck. | On the **Mekong**, facing **Thailand**; the **First Thai–Lao Friendship Bridge** (1994) crosses to Nong Khai. Sacked by Siam in **1827**. The **Pha That Luang** stupa is the national symbol, and the **Patuxai** arch was built with **US-donated cement** meant for an airport runway, earning it the nickname "**vertical runway**". |
| **Beirut** | Lebanon | From Phoenician/Canaanite ***be'erot***, "**wells**". | About 5,000 years old. Roman **Berytus** had a famous **school of law**. Called the "**Paris of the Middle East**" before the 1975–90 civil war; the **Green Line** divided the city. The **4 August 2020 port explosion** of ammonium nitrate was one of the largest non-nuclear blasts ever. |
| **Kuala Lumpur** | Malaysia | Malay, "**muddy confluence**", where the **Klang** and **Gombak** rivers meet. | Founded around **1857** as a **tin-mining** town by Chinese miners (Yap Ah Loy is called its founder). The **Petronas Towers** (452 m) were the **world's tallest buildings (1998–2004)**; **Merdeka 118** (679 m, 2023) is now the world's second-tallest. The federal government moved to **Putrajaya** (1999). |
| **Malé** | Maldives | Possibly from Sanskrit ***mahaalay***, "**great house**", or "**big island**".\* | One of the **most densely populated cities** on Earth, filling almost an entire island. The **Hulhumalé** artificial island was built nearby to relieve crowding; the **Sinamalé Bridge** (2018) links Malé to the airport island. The **Old Friday Mosque** is built of **coral stone**. |
| **Ulaanbaatar** | Mongolia | "**Red hero**", named in **1924** after revolutionary **Damdin Sükhbaatar**. Earlier **Urga** and **Ikh Khüree** ("great camp"). | Began in **1639** as a **moving monastery** town, settling in its current site only in 1778. The **coldest capital in the world** by average annual temperature (about **−1 °C**). A huge **Genghis Khan** statue sits in front of the parliament on **Sükhbaatar Square**. |
| **Naypyidaw** | Myanmar | "**Abode of kings**" or "royal capital". | Built in secret and announced as capital in **November 2005**, replacing **Yangon**. Famous for **20-lane highways** that are nearly empty. About **four times the size of London** in area. Hosted the **2013 SEA Games**. |
| **Kathmandu** | Nepal | From the **Kasthamandap** ("**wooden shelter**"), a pagoda in Durbar Square said to have been built from the wood of **a single sal tree**. It collapsed in the **2015 earthquake** and was rebuilt in 2021. | The **Kathmandu Valley** holds **seven UNESCO monuments**, including **Swayambhunath** (the "Monkey Temple"), **Boudhanath** and **Pashupatinath**. The hippie trail's end point in the 1960s–70s (**Freak Street**). Older name **Kantipur**. |
| **Pyongyang** | North Korea | "**Flat land**" or "**peaceful land**". | Capital of the ancient **Goguryeo** kingdom from **427**. Rebuilt almost entirely after US bombing in the **Korean War**. The **Ryugyong Hotel** (330 m, a pyramid) has never opened. Has one of the world's **deepest metros** (about **100 m** down), doubling as a bomb shelter. The **Rungrado May Day Stadium** is among the world's largest. |
| **Muscat** | Oman | Possibly "**anchorage**" or "**place of dropping anchor**".\* | Controlled by the **Portuguese** (1507–1650), who built the twin forts **Jalali** and **Mirani**. The **Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque** has one of the world's largest hand-woven carpets. Its harbour was a key Indian Ocean port linking Oman to **Zanzibar** and **Gwadar**. |
| **Islamabad** | Pakistan | "**City of Islam**" (abode of Islam). The name was suggested by a schoolteacher, **Qazi Abdur Rehman Amritsari**. | Planned by Greek architect **Constantinos Doxiadis** in the **1960s** to replace **Karachi**; laid out in lettered **sectors** (F-7, G-6 etc.). The **Faisal Mosque**, shaped like a **Bedouin tent**, was funded by **King Faisal of Saudi Arabia** and was the world's largest mosque from 1986 to 1993. **Rawalpindi**, the army headquarters, is its twin city. |
| **East Jerusalem** (claimed); **Ramallah** (administrative) | Palestine | See Israel. Ramallah is "**height of God**" (*ramah* + *Allah*). | Palestine claims **East Jerusalem** as its capital; the **Palestinian Authority** works from **Ramallah**, where **Yasser Arafat**'s tomb is in the **Muqata'a**. |
| **Manila** | Philippines | From Tagalog ***may-nilad***, "**where the nilad grows**", a mangrove shrub on the riverbank. | **Miguel López de Legazpi** founded Spanish Manila in **1571**. The **Manila galleons** linked it to **Acapulco** for 250 years, the first regular trans-Pacific trade. **Intramuros** is the walled old city. The **most densely populated city in the world**. The national capital region is **Metro Manila**; **Quezon City** was capital from 1948 to 1976. |
| **Doha** | Qatar | Arabic ***dawha***, "**big tree**" or "**bay**".\* | A small pearl-diving and fishing town until **oil and gas**. Hosted the **2022 FIFA World Cup**, the first in the Arab world. Home of **Al Jazeera** (1996), **Qatar Airways** and the **Museum of Islamic Art** by **I. M. Pei**. |
| **Riyadh** | Saudi Arabia | Arabic, "**gardens**" or "meadows", from its oases. | **Ibn Saud** captured the **Masmak Fort** in **1902**, the founding raid of modern Saudi Arabia. Landmarks: the **Kingdom Centre** (the "bottle opener") and **Al Faisaliyah**. Will host **Expo 2030**. Not to be confused with **Jeddah**, the main port and old diplomatic capital. |
| **Singapore** | Singapore | Sanskrit ***Singapura***, "**lion city**". Legend: Prince **Sang Nila Utama** saw a lion here (there were never lions in Singapore; it was probably a tiger). | A **city-state**. **Sir Stamford Raffles** founded the British trading post in **1819**. Expelled from Malaysia in **1965**. Iconic sights: the **Merlion**, **Marina Bay Sands** and **Gardens by the Bay**. **Changi Airport** regularly tops world rankings. Hosts the first **night race in F1** (2008). |
| **Seoul** | South Korea | A native Korean word meaning "**capital**". It is one of the few capitals whose name has **no Chinese characters**. Old names: **Hanyang** and **Hanseong**; under Japan **Keijō** (Gyeongseong). | Capital since **1394** (Joseon dynasty, under **King Taejo**). The **Gyeongbokgung** palace is the grandest. Only about **50 km from the DMZ**. Hosted the **1988 Olympics** and co-hosted the **2002 World Cup**. Half of South Korea's people live in the Seoul Capital Area. |
| **Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte** (official); **Colombo** (commercial) | Sri Lanka | Kotte: "**fortress city of resounding victory**", the 15th-century Kotte kingdom. Colombo: possibly from Sinhala ***Kolon thota***, "**port on the river Kelani**", reshaped by the Portuguese to honour **Columbus**.\* | Parliament moved to Kotte in **1982**. Colombo's **Lotus Tower** (350 m) is the tallest structure in South Asia. Colombo was successively controlled by the Portuguese, Dutch and British. |
| **Damascus** | Syria | An ancient name of uncertain meaning; in Arabic ***al-Sham*** or ***Dimashq***.\* | Often called the **oldest continuously inhabited capital**. **St Paul** was converted on the "**road to Damascus**". The **Umayyad Mosque** (715) is one of the oldest great mosques. Gave its name to **damask** cloth and **Damascus steel**. The capital of the Umayyad Caliphate (661–750). The **Assad** regime fell when rebels took the city in **December 2024**. |
| **Taipei** | Taiwan | "**North of Taiwan**" (*tai* + *pei*, north). | Capital of the **Republic of China** since **1949**, when **Chiang Kai-shek**'s government fled the mainland. **Taipei 101** (508 m) was the **world's tallest building (2004–10)** and has a giant **tuned mass damper** ball. The **National Palace Museum** holds treasures taken from Beijing's Forbidden City. |
| **Dushanbe** | Tajikistan | Tajik/Persian, "**Monday**": it grew from a village with a **Monday market** bazaar. Called **Stalinabad** (1929–61). | Has one of the world's **tallest flagpoles** (165 m, 2011). Lies below the **Pamir** and Hissar mountains. |
| **Bangkok** | Thailand | Probably from ***bang*** (village on a stream) + ***makok*** (a plum/olive tree). The Thai name **Krung Thep** begins a ceremonial name of **168 letters**, the **world's longest place name** in the Guinness book. | Became capital in **1782** under **King Rama I** (Chakri dynasty). Called the "**Venice of the East**" for its canals (*khlongs*). The **Grand Palace** holds the **Emerald Buddha**. Regularly the **world's most visited city** (by international arrivals). |
| **Dili** | Timor-Leste | Possibly from the local word for the **ai-dila** (**papaya**) tree.\* | Capital of **Portuguese Timor** from 1769. Occupied by **Indonesia** (1975–99). Timor-Leste became the first new nation of the 21st century (**2002**) and became **ASEAN's 11th member** in **October 2025**. The **Cristo Rei** statue (27 m) stands on a headland. |
| **Ankara** | Türkiye | Possibly Greek ***ankyra***, "**anchor**"; the Romans called it **Ancyra**.\* | **Atatürk** made it capital on **13 October 1923** instead of Istanbul, which was vulnerable and linked to the Ottoman past. Its **Costanera Center** (300 m) is the **tallest building in South America**. The **La Moneda** palace was bombed in **Pinochet**'s coup (**11 September 1973**). |
| **Bogotá** | Colombia | From **Bacatá**, the **Muisca** settlement, "**enclosure beyond the fields**".\* Called **Santa Fe de Bogotá** for centuries. | Founded by **Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada** in **1538**. At about **2,640 m**, one of the highest capitals. The **Gold Museum** has the famous **Muisca raft** (linked to the **El Dorado** legend). The **TransMilenio** bus system and **Ciclovía** car-free Sundays are copied worldwide. |
| **Quito** | Ecuador | From the **Quitu** people. | The **highest official capital** (about **2,850 m**) and the closest capital to the **Equator**; the **Mitad del Mundo** monument is just north. Its old town was one of the **first two UNESCO World Heritage cities** (**1978**, with Kraków). Ecuador is named after the Equator. |
| **Georgetown** | Guyana | After **King George III** (1812). Earlier **Stabroek** under the Dutch. | Much of it is **below sea level**, protected by a **sea wall** built by the Dutch. **St George's Cathedral** is one of the world's tallest **wooden churches**. Guyana is the only **English-speaking** country in South America and hosts the **CARICOM** secretariat. Huge **offshore oil** finds (since 2015) made it the world's fastest-growing economy. |
| **Asunción** | Paraguay | From the **Feast of the Assumption** (*Asunción*) of the Virgin Mary, on which it was founded (**15 August 1537**). | One of the **oldest cities in South America**, called "**Mother of Cities**" because expeditions from here founded others, including **Buenos Aires**' second founding. Paraguay is **bilingual** in **Spanish** and **Guaraní**. |
| **Lima** | Peru | From the **Rímac** River, via Quechua ***Limaq***, "**the talker**".\* Pizarro named it **Ciudad de los Reyes** ("**City of the Kings**", for the **Three Kings**). | Founded by **Francisco Pizarro** on **18 January 1535**. The capital of the Spanish **Viceroyalty of Peru**. One of the driest capitals (it almost never rains, but is often covered by **garúa** fog). Famous for **ceviche** and as a food capital. |
| **Paramaribo** | Suriname | Possibly from a native village name, "**inhabitants of the Para river**".\* | The **Dutch** traded **New Amsterdam (New York)** to the British for Suriname in **1667** (**Treaty of Breda**). Its **wooden colonial centre** is a UNESCO site. A **mosque and synagogue** stand side by side on Keizerstraat. The only **Dutch-speaking** country in South America. |
| **Montevideo** | Uruguay | Popularly from a sailor's cry "**Monte vide eu**" ("I see a mountain"), but more likely from a navigation note "**Monte VI De Este a Oeste**" (the sixth mountain from east to west).\* | Founded by the Spanish in **1724–26**. Hosted the **first FIFA World Cup** (**1930**) at the **Estadio Centenario**. The **southernmost capital in the Americas**. The **Rambla** waterfront runs for over 20 km. |
| **Caracas** | Venezuela | From the **Caracas** people, who lived in the valley. Founded as **Santiago de León de Caracas** (**1567**). | Birthplace of **Simón Bolívar**, "**El Libertador**" (**1783**). Lies in a valley below **Mount Ávila** (El Ávila). Known for **El Sistema** (youth orchestras) and the **Torre de David**, an unfinished skyscraper that became a squat. |

## Oceania

| Capital | Country | Name: origin and meaning | History and trivia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Canberra** | Australia | Probably from an **Ngunnawal** word for a **meeting place**; the name of a local farm, "**Canberry**".\* | Chosen in **1908** as a compromise between rivals **Sydney** and **Melbourne**; named on **12 March 1913** by **Lady Denman**. Designed by American architects **Walter Burley Griffin** and **Marion Mahony Griffin**, winners of a world competition. Parliament moved from **Melbourne** in **1927**. Built around **Lake Burley Griffin**. |
| **Suva** | Fiji | Origin uncertain; possibly from a Fijian word for a **mound**.\* | Replaced **Levuka** as capital in **1882**. The largest city in the South Pacific outside Australia and New Zealand. Home of the **University of the South Pacific** and the **Pacific Islands Forum** secretariat. |
| **South Tarawa** | Kiribati | Tarawa is the atoll's name; Kiribati is the local spelling of "**Gilberts**" (pronounced "Kiri-bas"). | The **Battle of Tarawa** (**November 1943**) was one of the bloodiest of the Pacific war. Kiribati spans all four hemispheres and moved the **International Date Line** in **1995**, so its **Line Islands** are the first place on Earth to see each new day. |
| **Majuro** | Marshall Islands | The atoll's name. | A narrow atoll; most of the land is under **3 m** above sea level, making it highly vulnerable to **sea-level rise**. The US tested nuclear weapons at **Bikini** and **Enewetak** (1946–58); the **bikini** swimsuit was named after Bikini Atoll. |
| **Palikir** | Micronesia | A village name on **Pohnpei**. | Replaced **Kolonia** in **1989**. Pohnpei has the ruins of **Nan Madol**, an ancient city built on artificial islets. Yap island uses giant stone discs, **rai stones**, as money. |
| **Yaren** (no official capital) | Nauru | A district name. | Nauru is the **world's smallest island country** and the **only country with no official capital**. Its **phosphate** mining once made it one of the richest countries per head; it is now home to Australia's offshore **detention centre**. |
| **Wellington** | New Zealand | After **Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington**, the victor of **Waterloo**. Its Māori name is ***Te Whanganui-a-Tara*** ("the great harbour of Tara"). | Replaced **Auckland** as capital in **1865** because of its central location. The **southernmost capital** of a sovereign state. Nicknamed "**Windy Wellington**". The **Beehive** houses ministers. A base of **Weta Workshop** (*The Lord of the Rings*); called "**Wellywood**". |
| **Ngerulmud** | Palau | Palauan for the place where **women offered medicinal plants** to the gods.\* | The government moved from **Koror** in **2006**. Possibly the **least populated capital** of any sovereign state (a few hundred people at most). Its **Capitol** looks like the **US Capitol**. Palau created one of the first national **shark sanctuaries** (2009). |
| **Port Moresby** | Papua New Guinea | After **Admiral Fairfax Moresby**, father of **Captain John Moresby**, who explored the harbour in **1873**. | The **Kokoda Track** (WWII) starts nearby. PNG has the **most languages** of any country (over **800**). The Parliament House is built like a **haus tambaran** (spirit house). |
| **Apia** | Samoa | A village name. | **Robert Louis Stevenson** lived and died here (**1894**) at **Vailima**; the Samoans called him ***Tusitala*** ("teller of tales"). Samoa **skipped 30 December 2011** to move west of the **Date Line**. |
| **Honiara** | Solomon Islands | From ***nagho ni ara***, "**facing the east and south-east wind**".\* | Built on the site of a US military base after the **Battle of Guadalcanal** (**1942–43**), the first major Allied offensive in the Pacific. Replaced **Tulagi**, destroyed in the war. Hosted the **2023 Pacific Games**. |
| **Nukuʻalofa** | Tonga | "**Abode of love**" (*nuku* abode + *ʻalofa* love). | Tonga is the Pacific's **only kingdom** that was **never colonised**. The **Royal Palace** (1867) is a wooden Victorian building made in **New Zealand**. The **Hunga Tonga–Hunga Haʻapai** eruption (**January 2022**) was the largest volcanic blast in over a century. |
| **Funafuti** | Tuvalu | The atoll's name. | One of the smallest capitals. The airport runway doubles as a **football field and sports ground**. Tuvalu earns money from the internet domain **.tv**. Under the **Falepili Union** (2023) Australia offered climate visas to Tuvaluans. |
| **Port Vila** | Vanuatu | "**Port**" + **Efil**/Ifira, the local name. | The New Hebrides was ruled jointly by Britain and France as a **condominium** (1906–80), with **two police forces, two prisons and two currencies**. Vanuatu's ***Bislama*** is a national language. |
