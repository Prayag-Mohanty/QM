Carmakers run some of the most systematic naming traditions in business, and the systems themselves are the quiz material: winds at Volkswagen and Maserati, fighting bulls at Lamborghini, crowns at Toyota, dynasties at BYD. This page covers the marques first, then the model names.

## Quiz-ready stories

1. **Audi** is Latin for "listen". Founder **August Horch** was barred from reusing his own surname after a boardroom split, so he translated it — *horch* is German for "listen".
2. **Porsche's 911** was launched as the **901**. **Peugeot** held French trademark rights to every three-digit name with a zero in the middle, so Porsche swapped the 0 for a 1 and reused the badges it had already cast.
3. **Lamborghini** names its cars after **fighting bulls**. Ferruccio Lamborghini built **tractors** first and only made cars after Enzo Ferrari told him to stick to them.
4. **Volvo** is Latin for "**I roll**" — it began as a subsidiary of ball-bearing maker SKF.
5. **Volkswagen** names cars after **winds**: Golf is the *Gulf Stream*, Passat is the trade wind, Jetta the jet stream, Scirocco the Saharan wind.
6. **Toyota** was spelt **Toyoda** after the founding family. It was changed because "Toyota" takes **eight** katakana brushstrokes, a luckier number than ten.
7. **Subaru** is Japanese for the **Pleiades**; the six stars are the six firms that merged into Fuji Heavy Industries.
8. **Mahindra** was founded as **Mahindra & Mohammed**. The third partner, Malik Ghulam Muhammad, left at Partition and became **Pakistan's first finance minister**.
9. **Tesla's** lineup was meant to spell **S-E-X-Y**. Ford owned "Model E", so the 3 is a stylised E.
10. **Jaguar** sold cars as **SS Cars** until the end of the Second World War, when the initials became unusable.
11. **Ferrari's** Prancing Horse was the personal emblem of WWI fighter ace **Francesco Baracca**; his mother gave Enzo permission to use it.
12. **Adidas and Puma** were one company, founded by the **Dassler brothers** in Herzogenaurach, split by a family feud in 1948. (Not a car, but the same town-splitting rivalry logic.)

## Brand names and their origins

### American

| Brand | Name origin / founder |
| --- | --- |
| **Ford** | Henry Ford, 1903. First to pair mass production with a wage high enough ($5/day, 1914) that workers could buy the cars. |
| **Chevrolet** | Swiss racer **Louis Chevrolet** with ousted GM founder William Durant. The bowtie's origin is disputed: Paris hotel wallpaper, or a stylised Swiss cross. |
| **Cadillac** | **Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac**, the Frenchman who founded Detroit in 1701. The crest is his family coat of arms. |
| **Buick** | **David Dunbar Buick**, Scottish-born, who made his first fortune bonding porcelain enamel to cast iron — for bathtubs. |
| **Lincoln** | Named after Abraham Lincoln by founder **Henry Leland**, the first president he ever voted for. Leland had earlier founded Cadillac. |
| **Dodge** | Brothers **John and Horace Dodge**, who spent two decades as Ford's parts supplier before building their own cars. |
| **Jeep** | Contested: the military "GP" (General Purpose), or **Eugene the Jeep** from the Popeye comic strip. |
| **Tesla** | Nikola Tesla. Founded by **Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning**; Musk joined in 2004 and won co-founder status in a 2009 settlement. |
| **Rivian** | The **Indian River** near founder RJ Scaringe's Florida hometown. |

### German

| Brand | Name origin / founder |
| --- | --- |
| **Volkswagen** | "People's car", 1937, a Nazi-era state project. The Beetle was designed by Ferdinand Porsche. |
| **Audi** | Latin for "listen" — a translation of founder **August Horch's** surname. The four rings are the 1932 merger of Audi, DKW, Horch and Wanderer. |
| **BMW** | *Bayerische Motoren Werke*, founded 1916 to build **aircraft engines**. The roundel is Bavaria's blue-and-white colours, not a propeller. |
| **Mercedes-Benz** | **Mercédès Jellinek**, daughter of the businessman who insisted Daimler sell under her name, plus **Karl Benz**. Merged 1926. |
| **Porsche** | Ferdinand Porsche's engineering consultancy, 1931 — it designed the VW Beetle before building its own cars. |
| **Smart** | An acronym: **S**watch **M**ercedes **ART** — a watchmaker-carmaker joint venture. |
| **Opel** | **Adam Opel**, who made **sewing machines** before bicycles, then cars. |

### Stellantis, France and Italy

| Brand | Name origin / founder |
| --- | --- |
| **Fiat** | *Fabbrica Italiana Automobili Torino*, 1899. |
| **Peugeot** | Made **coffee grinders and saws** before cars; the lion logo (1858) first symbolised its steel tools. |
| **Citroën** | The double chevron is a stylised **double-helical gear tooth**, founder André Citroën's patented invention. |
| **Alfa Romeo** | ALFA (1910) plus engineer **Nicola Romeo**, who took over in 1915. |
| **Maserati** | The Maserati brothers. The trident comes from the **Fountain of Neptune** in Bologna. |
| **Abarth** | Carlo Abarth. The scorpion is **his own star sign**. |
| **Ferrari** | Enzo Ferrari. The Prancing Horse came from fighter ace Francesco Baracca. Spun out of Fiat in 2015–16. |
| **Renault / Alpine / Dacia** | The Renault brothers; Alpine for Jean Rédélé's Alpine racing; Dacia for the **Roman province** that is now Romania. |

### Japan and Korea

| Brand | Name origin / founder |
| --- | --- |
| **Toyota** | From **Toyoda Automatic Loom Works**. Respelt for the lucky eight-stroke count. The loom company still exists and is a major Toyota shareholder. |
| **Nissan** | Short for **Nippon Sangyo**, "Japan Industries". |
| **Mazda** | Officially **Ahura Mazda**, the Zoroastrian god of wisdom — conveniently close to founder **Jujiro Matsuda's** name. |
| **Subaru** | The **Pleiades** star cluster; six stars for six merged companies. |
| **Mitsubishi** | "Three diamonds" — founder Iwasaki Yataro's family crest combined with his patron clan's. |
| **Suzuki** | **Michio Suzuki**, another loom manufacturer who pivoted to vehicles. |
| **Isuzu** | The **Isuzu River**, which flows past the Ise Grand Shrine. |
| **Hyundai** | Korean for "**modernity**". Founder Chung Ju-yung's firm was a **construction** company that built the Gyeongbu Expressway. |
| **Kia** | *Ki* ("to arise") + *A* for Asia — "arise out of Asia". |
| **SsangYong** | "**Twin dragons**". |

### Britain, Sweden and the rest

| Brand | Name origin / founder |
| --- | --- |
| **Rolls-Royce** | Aristocrat dealer **Charles Rolls** and engineer **Henry Royce**, 1904. The Spirit of Ecstasy was sculpted in 1911. |
| **Bentley** | **W.O. Bentley**, a former WWI aero-engine designer. |
| **Aston Martin** | Founder **Lionel Martin** plus the **Aston Clinton** hillclimb he raced at. |
| **McLaren** | New Zealand F1 driver **Bruce McLaren**. |
| **Jaguar** | Sold as **SS Cars** until WWII made the initials impossible; renamed for its own model. |
| **Mini** | Designed by Alec Issigonis for BMC in 1959; a standalone BMW brand only from 2000. |
| **Volvo** | Latin "**I roll**", from ball-bearing parent SKF. |
| **Koenigsegg / Polestar** | Founder's surname; Polestar began as a racing team, named for the North Star. |

### China and India

| Brand | Name origin / founder |
| --- | --- |
| **BYD** | "Build Your Dreams" is a **retrofitted English backronym**; the letters come from the pinyin of the original Chinese name, Biyadi. |
| **Geely** | Chinese for "**lucky / auspicious**". Owns Volvo, Polestar, Lotus and most of Proton. |
| **Great Wall (GWM)** | The Great Wall. Its sub-brand **Wey** is named after GWM's own chairman, **Wei Jianjun**. |
| **SAIC** | Shanghai Automotive. Owns **MG** — the initials still stand for **Morris Garages** — bought from the Rover wreckage in 2007. |
| **Hongqi** | "**Red Flag**", FAW's marque, founded 1958 to build ceremonial cars for China's leadership. |
| **Tata Motors** | Tata Group (1868). Bought **Jaguar Land Rover** in 2008. |
| **Mahindra** | Founded 1945 as **Mahindra & Mohammed**; renamed after the third partner emigrated at Partition and became Pakistan's first finance minister. |
| **Maruti** | Another name for **Hanuman**, the wind god. |
| **Proton / Perodua** | *Perusahaan Otomobil Nasional* and *Perusahaan Otomobil Kedua* — "National" and literally "**Second**" automobile company. |

### Defunct marques

| Brand | Hook |
| --- | --- |
| **Pontiac** | **Chief Pontiac**, the Odawa leader who led a rising against British rule. |
| **Oldsmobile** | **Ransom E. Olds**, a mass-production pioneer who predates Ford's assembly line. |

| **Studebaker** | Began in **1852** as a **wagon maker** for the US Army — half a century before it built a car. |

## Model naming systems

The system is usually the question, not the individual car.

| Maker | System | Hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Volkswagen** | **Winds and currents** | Golf = *Gulfstrom*, Passat = trade wind, Jetta = jet stream, Scirocco = Saharan wind, Bora = Adriatic wind. **Tiguan** is *Tiger* + *Leguan* (iguana), picked by a **magazine readers' poll**. The Golf was sold in the US as the **Rabbit**. |
| **Maserati** | **Winds** (predates VW by decades) | Mistral, Ghibli, Bora, Levante, Khamsin, Shamal. **Quattroporte** just means "four doors"; **Sebring** and **Mexico** commemorate race wins. |
| **Lamborghini** | **Fighting bulls** | **Murciélago** was a bull spared in 1879 after surviving 24 sword strikes. **Huracán** and **Aventador** were also famous bulls. **Urus** is the aurochs. The outlier is **Countach**, a Piedmontese exclamation meaning roughly "wow". |
| **Toyota** | **Crowns** | **Corolla** is Latin for "little crown", **Corona** is "crown", **Camry** anglicises *kanmuri* (冠), "crown" — all orbiting the actual **Crown**. Also **RAV4** = Recreational Active Vehicle 4WD; **Hilux** = High + Luxury; **Prius** = Latin "before". |
| **Porsche** | **Numbers, then predators** | The **901 → 911** trademark story. **Macan** is Indonesian for tiger; **Taycan** is roughly "soul of a young horse", nodding to the crest. |
| **Ferrari** | **Displacement per cylinder** | The first Ferrari, the **125 S**, had a 1500cc V12: 1500 ÷ 12 = 125. **F40** and **F50** mark anniversaries; **SF90** marks Scuderia Ferrari's 90th. **LaFerrari** means "The Ferrari" and was codenamed **F150** — the same as Ford's pickup. |
| **Mercedes-Benz** | **German segment words** | **S**-Class from *Sonderklasse* ("special class"); **G**-Wagen from *Geländewagen* ("terrain vehicle"). |
| **BYD** | **Two parallel systems** | Chinese-market cars are named after **dynasties** (Han, Tang, Song, Yuan, Qin); export models after **sea creatures** (Seal, Dolphin, Shark). |
| **Aston Martin** | **DB** | The initials of **David Brown**, who bought the company in 1947 — still used decades after his ownership ended. |
| **Volvo** | **Letters** | S = sedan, V = versatility (wagon), XC = cross country. |
| **McLaren** | **Horsepower** | 720S, 750S, 765LT correspond to PS output. |
| **Hyundai / Kia** | **American places** | Santa Fe, Tucson, Telluride — aspirational US-West names on Korean cars. |
| **Chevrolet** | **Mixed** | **Corvette** is a small, fast **naval warship** class. **Camaro** was announced as an archaic French word for "friend" — no such word exists; it is widely read as invented. |
| **Ford** | **Horses** | **Mustang's** origin is disputed: designer John Najjar first pitched the **P-51 Mustang fighter plane** and was refused, then re-pitched the wild horse. **Bronco** continued the theme. |
| **Tesla** | **S3XY** | Ford's "Model E" trademark forced the stylised 3. |
| **Land Rover** | **Wheelbase, then marketing** | The Defender was just the "Ninety" and "One Ten" (inches) until **1990**, when the name was invented. It was never designed as a military vehicle. |
| **Peugeot** | **Zero in the middle** | 201, 208, 308, 508 — the convention since **1929**, and the reason the Porsche 901 had to be renamed. |
