**Major Chandrakant (Chandrakanth) Nair** is a former Army medical officer turned quizmaster, based in Kerala, and one of the busiest on the Indian circuit. He gave a TEDx talk called *The Question of a Question*. This guide is built from **79 of his questions** across four sets: the Christ-Com Quiz 2026 prelims, semifinals and finals (business) and the Monty Quiz 8.0 prelims (general).

## How he sets questions

He rarely asks a bare fact. A typical question gives **two or three short clues from different worlds** and asks for the **name that links them**, often a two-part answer ("Name the city and the beer"), sometimes with letter counts as a hint. Most answers can be **worked out** from the clues if you think about the link before you start recalling.

## His round formats

- **Written prelims** of 15–16 questions, almost every one with a **picture slide** (a logo, an ad, a film still, a painting).
- **Clockwise and anti-clockwise** direct-and-pass rounds in the stages.
- **Themed written rounds**: in the Christ-Com finals, a **name-origins** round and an **ads-and-campaigns** round.
- **Two-part answers** are common ("Name the service and the film"; "the city and the beer, 6 + 5"). Getting half often earns nothing, so read the question twice.
- He likes a **visual connect**: the answer is often shown on the picture slide that follows.

## How to prepare for a Major Nair quiz

1. **Re-read his past sets** (above) and note the patterns, not the answers.
2. **Name origins**: take 50 well-known brands and learn why each is called what it is. This is his single biggest category.
3. **Logos and colours**: Indian banks, PSUs, airlines and sports clubs; trademarked colours.
4. **Ads**: famous Indian and global campaigns, Super Bowl ads, stunts that took a slogan literally.
5. **Firsts and inventions**: the first ATM, barcode, credit card, online sale, and so on.
6. **The last 12 months**: big business deals, renames, viral moments and deaths, each tied to an older fact.
7. **The host city**: its companies, founders, crafts, teams and famous people.
8. **In the quiz**: find the link between the clues first, then recall. Watch for two-part answers and letter counts.

Two quick drills: (1) for 20 global brands, name one other thing that shares the name; (2) for 20 Indian companies, say the founder, year and the story behind the name aloud.

## Practice set in his style

Twelve questions written the way he writes them. Try each in 45 seconds, then check the answers below.

1. In 1932 a Danish carpenter named his toy company after two words meaning "play well". Latin speakers later noticed the name also means "I put together". Name the company.
2. A coffee chain founded in Seattle in 1971 took its name from the first mate of the *Pequod*. Name the chain and the novel.
3. A sportswear brand is named after the Greek goddess of victory. Its logo was designed by a student, Carolyn Davidson, for $35. Name the brand and the logo.
4. A Swedish car maker's name is Latin for "I roll". Name it.
5. A chocolate's peak sits over a Swiss mountain; look closely and a bear is hidden in it, a nod to the city where the bar was made. Name the chocolate, the mountain and the city.
6. On 26 June 1974, in Troy, Ohio, a pack of chewing gum became the first product sold using a new technology. Which technology, and which gum?
7. A 1960s ice-cream maker from the Bronx gave his brand a made-up Danish-sounding name to suggest old-world quality. Name it.
8. A drink invented by a pharmacist in North Carolina was named for its supposed power to cure an upset stomach. Name the drink and the condition its name comes from.
9. A sportswear brand founded as J.W. Foster and Sons was renamed in 1958 after an African antelope. Name the brand.
10. A Japanese electronics company combined the Latin for "sound" with an American slang word for a young boy. Name it.
11. Since 1966, a small girl in a polka-dot frock has commented on India's news in hoardings. Name the brand, and the agency founder who created her.
12. A Mexican beer and a family of viruses share a name meaning "crown". Name it.

**Answers**

1. LEGO (*leg godt*).
2. Starbucks; *Moby-Dick*.
3. Nike; the Swoosh.
4. Volvo.
5. Toblerone; the Matterhorn; Bern.
6. The barcode (UPC scan); Wrigley's Juicy Fruit.
7. Häagen-Dazs (Reuben Mattus).
8. Pepsi; dyspepsia.
9. Reebok (the rhebok antelope).
10. Sony (*sonus* + "sonny").
11. Amul; Sylvester daCunha (the Amul girl, "Utterly Butterly Delicious").
12. Corona.
