This page is the quiz-night material: records, oddities, patterns and stories. Facts are from general knowledge; the stories behind old flags are often legend, so the best-known version is given.\*

## Records and one-offs

- **Oldest flag in continuous use**: Denmark's **Dannebrog**. Legend says it fell from the sky during the **Battle of Lyndanisse (1219)** in Estonia. It's the model for every **Nordic cross** flag.
- **Only non-rectangular national flag**: **Nepal**, two stacked pennants for the Himalayas (or the two branches of the ruling dynasty). Its **constitution** gives step-by-step geometric instructions for drawing it.
- **Square flags**: only **Switzerland** and **Vatican City**.
- **Different on each side**: **Paraguay**. The front shows the national coat of arms, the back the **Treasury seal** (a lion and the cap of liberty, "Paz y Justicia").
- **Never flown at half-mast**: **Saudi Arabia**, because it carries the **Shahada** (the Islamic declaration of faith). It's made double-sided so the text reads correctly from both sides. **Afghanistan's** Taliban flag has the same rule.
- **Only plain single-colour flag**: **Libya**'s all-green flag under **Gaddafi** (1977–2011). Libya went back to its 1951 independence flag after his fall.
- **A modern rifle on a flag**: **Mozambique**, an **AK-47** with a bayonet, crossed with a hoe on an open book.
- **Maps on flags**: **Cyprus** (the island, in copper-orange, for its copper) and **Kosovo** (its outline, under six stars for its peoples).
- **People on a flag**: **Belize**, two woodcutters on its coat of arms. It also has one of the **most colours** of any flag (12).
- **Purple**, the rarest flag colour: **Dominica** (the **Sisserou parrot**) and **Nicaragua** (a rainbow in the coat of arms).
- **Buildings on flags**: **Cambodia** (**Angkor Wat**, the only national flag with a building at its centre), plus **Afghanistan**'s old flags (a mosque), **Portugal** (castles in the shield) and **San Marino** (three towers).
- **Birds on flags**: Uganda (**grey crowned crane**), Papua New Guinea (**raggiana bird of paradise**), Dominica (Sisserou parrot), Zambia (**African fish eagle**), Kiribati (**frigatebird**), Zimbabwe (the **Zimbabwe Bird**), Mexico (golden eagle), Albania (double-headed eagle).
- **Designed by teenagers**: **Papua New Guinea**'s flag by **Susan Karike**, aged **15** (1971); the **US 50-star flag** by **Robert Heft**, aged **17**, as a school project that famously got a **B−**.
- **The biggest number of stars**: the **United States** (50).

## Lookalikes (classic "identify the flag" traps)

- **Chad and Romania**: blue–yellow–red vertical tricolours; Chad's blue is slightly darker. Romania's dates from 1848, Chad's from 1959.
- **Indonesia and Monaco**: both red over white; they differ only in **proportions** (Indonesia 2:3, Monaco 4:5). **Poland** is the reverse, white over red.
- **Netherlands and Luxembourg**: red–white–blue; Luxembourg's blue is lighter and the flag longer. The Dutch flag began as orange–white–blue (the **Prince's Flag** of William of Orange).
- **Ireland and Côte d'Ivoire**: the same three colours in **reverse** order (Ireland: green–white–orange from the hoist). Italy swaps orange for red.
- **Australia and New Zealand**: both blue ensigns with the Southern Cross. Australia's stars are **white** (plus a 7-pointed **Commonwealth Star**); New Zealand's are **red with white borders**, only four. NZ voted to keep its flag in **2015–16 referendums** (the silver fern design lost).
- **Senegal and Mali**: green–yellow–red; Senegal has a **green star** in the middle.
- **Norway and Iceland**: Nordic crosses with reversed colours (Norway red with blue cross; Iceland blue with red cross).
- **Colombia, Ecuador and Venezuela**: yellow–blue–red from **Gran Colombia** (Francisco de Miranda's flag). Colombia's yellow band is double width; Ecuador adds the coat of arms; Venezuela adds an arc of **eight stars**.
- **Slovenia, Slovakia and Russia**: white–blue–red; Slovenia and Slovakia add their coats of arms.

## Colour families

- **Pan-African** (green, yellow, red): from **Ethiopia**, the African nation never colonised. **Ghana** was the first newly independent African state to use them (1957), adding the **Black Star** from **Marcus Garvey**'s Black Star Line.
- **Pan-African (UNIA)** red, black and green: **Marcus Garvey**'s 1920 flag, seen in **Kenya** and **Malawi**.
- **Pan-Arab** (black, white, green, red): from the **Arab Revolt flag** of **1916** against the Ottomans. Used by Jordan, Palestine, Sudan, Kuwait, the UAE, Iraq, Syria and Egypt.
- **Pan-Slavic** (white, blue, red): from the **Russian** tricolour, which **Peter the Great** is said to have based on the Dutch flag. Adopted by Slavs at the **Prague Slavic Congress (1848)**.
- **Nordic cross**: Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Iceland (and the Faroe and Åland islands). The cross is off-centre, towards the hoist.
- **Central American** blue–white–blue: from the **Federal Republic of Central America** (1823–41). Used by **Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Guatemala** (vertical stripes) and **Costa Rica** (with a red band added in 1848, inspired by France's revolution).

## Stars, crescents and the sky

- **The Southern Cross** appears on the flags of **Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea, Samoa and Brazil**.
- **Brazil**'s blue globe shows the **sky over Rio de Janeiro on 15 November 1889**, the morning the republic was proclaimed. Each of the **27 stars** stands for a state or the Federal District (the lone star above the band is **Pará**). The motto **"Ordem e Progresso"** comes from **Auguste Comte**'s positivism.
- **Turkey**: legend says the crescent and star were a reflection in a pool of blood after the **Battle of Kosovo (1389)**. The Ottoman flag inspired many Muslim-country flags.
- **Crescent flags** include Pakistan, Turkey, Algeria, Tunisia, Malaysia, Singapore, Mauritania, Azerbaijan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Libya, the Comoros and the Maldives. **Singapore**'s crescent stands for a young nation on the rise, not Islam.
- **Japan**: the **Hinomaru** ("circle of the sun") was used for centuries but only became the official flag by law in **1999**.
- **Bangladesh**: the red disc (the sun, and the blood of the 1971 war) is set **slightly towards the hoist**, so it looks centred when the flag flies.
- **Kiribati**: a frigatebird over the sun rising from the Pacific.
- **Argentina and Uruguay**: the **Sun of May** (*Sol de Mayo*), for the **May Revolution of 1810**.
- **Philippines**: an eight-rayed sun for the **eight provinces** that first revolted against Spain. The flag is flown **red side up in wartime**.

## Flags with a story

- **India**: designed by **Pingali Venkayya**, adopted **22 July 1947**. The **Ashoka Chakra** has **24 spokes**. Only **khadi** flags were allowed until the **Flag Code** was relaxed (2002, then polyester allowed in 2022). The **Naveen Jindal** case (2004) made flying the flag a fundamental right.
- **Pakistan**: designed by **Syed Amir-uddin Kedwaii**. Green for the Muslim majority, the white band for minorities.
- **Sri Lanka**: the **Lion Flag**, with four **Bo leaves** in the corners for Buddhist virtues; orange and green stripes for Tamils and Muslims.
- **Bhutan**: the white **Druk** (thunder dragon) holding jewels; *Druk Yul* means "Land of the Thunder Dragon".
- **South Korea**: the **Taegukgeuk** (yin-yang) and four **trigrams** of the *I Ching* (heaven, earth, water, fire).
- **Israel**: inspired by the **tallit** (prayer shawl), with the **Star of David**. It grew from the flag of the Zionist movement, shown at the **First Zionist Congress (1897)** and credited to **David Wolffsohn**.
- **Canada**: the **Maple Leaf** (1965), proposed by historian **George Stanley**, after the "Great Flag Debate". It replaced the Red Ensign.
- **United Kingdom**: the **Union Jack** combines the crosses of **St George** (England), **St Andrew** (Scotland) and **St Patrick** (Ireland). **Wales** isn't on it.
- **United States**: 13 stripes for the original colonies, 50 stars for states. The current flag dates from **4 July 1960**, after Hawaii joined.
- **Mexico**: the eagle on a cactus eating a snake recalls the **Aztec legend** of where to found **Tenochtitlan**, today's Mexico City.
- **South Africa**: designed by **Frederick Brownell** for the **1994** election in about three weeks; the green **Y** shape stands for diverse peoples coming together.
- **Turkmenistan**: five **carpet medallions (guls)** for its five main tribes, plus an olive branch for its neutrality.
- **Kazakhstan**: a golden sun and **steppe eagle** on sky blue, with a national ornament along the hoist. Designed by **Shaken Niyazbekov** (1992).
- **Mongolia**: the **Soyombo**, a column of symbols (fire, sun, moon, yin-yang), also used as a script character.
- **Lebanon**: a green **cedar** between red bands.
- **Cyprus**: the white field and olive branches were chosen to be neutral between Greek and Turkish Cypriots.
- **Georgia**: the five-cross flag, a medieval design revived after the **Rose Revolution** (2004).

## Coats of arms and emblems

- **India**: the **Lion Capital of Ashoka** from **Sarnath**, adopted on **26 January 1950**. Only three of the four lions are visible. The motto **Satyameva Jayate** ("Truth alone triumphs") is from the **Mundaka Upanishad**.
- **United States**: the **Great Seal** has a bald eagle holding **13 arrows** and an olive branch with **13 leaves**. The motto **E pluribus unum** ("Out of many, one") has **13 letters**. Its reverse (the **unfinished pyramid** with the **Eye of Providence**) is on the **one-dollar bill**.
- **United Kingdom**: the royal arms have the **lion** (England) and the **unicorn** (Scotland, whose national animal it is), with the motto ***Dieu et mon droit*** ("God and my right"). In Scotland a different version gives Scotland the senior place.
- **Russia, Serbia, Montenegro, Albania**: **double-headed eagles**, from **Byzantium**. Albania's recalls **Skanderbeg**'s revolt against the Ottomans.
- **Germany**: the **Bundesadler** (federal eagle). The big version in the Bundestag is nicknamed the "**fat hen**" (*fette Henne*).
- **Austria**: an eagle holding a **sickle and hammer**, with **broken chains** added in 1945 for liberation from Nazi rule.
- **Japan** has **no official coat of arms**. The **chrysanthemum** seal is the Emperor's, and the **paulownia** seal is used by the government and the Prime Minister.
- **Israel**: a **menorah**, modelled on the one on the **Arch of Titus** in Rome, between olive branches.
- **Australia**: a shield held by a **kangaroo** and an **emu**, often said to be chosen because neither can easily walk backwards (a popular claim).
- **Canada**: the motto ***A mari usque ad mare*** ("From sea to sea").
- **South Africa** (2000): its motto is in the extinct **ǀXam** (Khoisan) language, ***!ke e: ǀxarra ǁke***, "Diverse people unite".
- **Zimbabwe**: the **Zimbabwe Bird**, from soapstone birds found at **Great Zimbabwe**; Germany returned one taken in colonial times (2003).
- **Vatican City**: the crossed **keys of Saint Peter** (gold and silver: power in heaven and on earth) under the papal tiara.
- **San Marino**: three towers on **Monte Titano**. The world's oldest surviving republic (founded traditionally in **301 CE**).
- **Mexico, Iran, Saudi Arabia**: Mexico's emblem is the eagle-and-snake; Iran's is a stylised **"Allah"** that also reads as a tulip (martyrdom); Saudi Arabia's is a **date palm over two crossed swords**.
- **Nepal**: the emblem shows Mount Everest, green hills, a map of Nepal, two hands shaking (gender equality) and a garland of **rhododendrons**, the national flower.
- **Sri Lanka**: a lion holding a sword, in a ring of **lotus petals**, above a **Dharmachakra**.
- **Ghana**: the coat of arms has **Osu Castle** (old seat of government) and a **cocoa tree**.
- **Kenya**: two lions holding spears and a shield, with the motto **Harambee** ("Let's pull together").

## National symbols beyond the flag

- **Scotland's** national animal is the **unicorn**; **Wales**'s flag is the **red dragon** (Y Ddraig Goch).
- **National flowers**: India's **lotus**, Japan's **cherry blossom** (and chrysanthemum), the Netherlands' **tulip**, Canada's **maple** (a tree), Pakistan's **jasmine**, England's **rose**, Scotland's **thistle**, Wales's **daffodil** and **leek**, Ireland's **shamrock**.
- **National animals**: India's **Bengal tiger**, the US **bald eagle**, Australia's **kangaroo**, New Zealand's **kiwi**, China's **giant panda**, Indonesia's **Komodo dragon**.
- **Marianne** (France) and **Lady Liberty** / **Columbia** (US) are national **personifications**; **Britannia** for the UK and **Bharat Mata** for India.

## Recent flag changes

- **Syria** (2024–25): after Assad fell in **December 2024**, the **independence flag** (green–white–black with three red stars) replaced the red–white–black two-star flag.
- **Afghanistan** (2021): the Taliban's white flag with the Shahada replaced the black–red–green tricolour.
- **Libya** (2011): back to the red–black–green independence flag with crescent and star.
- **Myanmar** (2010): a yellow–green–red tricolour with a large white star.
- **Iraq** (2008): removed the three green stars of the Ba'ath party, leaving *Allahu Akbar* in green Kufic script.
- **Rwanda** (2001): dropped red (associated with the genocide) for blue, yellow and green with a sun.
- **Georgia** (2004) and **South Africa** (1994): see above.
- **Fiji** planned to drop the Union Jack (2015) but dropped the plan in **2016**; **New Zealand** kept its flag after two referendums (2015–16).

## Flag words (vexillology)

- **Vexillology** is the study of flags, from Latin *vexillum*; the term was coined by **Whitney Smith** (1957).
- **Hoist**: the half next to the pole. **Fly**: the free end. **Canton**: the upper corner at the hoist (where the US stars and Australia's Union Jack sit). **Field**: the background.
- **Half-mast** (or half-staff): flown below the top as a sign of mourning; first raised to the top, then lowered.
- **Ensign**: a flag flown by ships to show nationality; **jack**: a small flag at the bow.
- **Vexillum**: the Roman cavalry standard the word comes from.
