## The Economist

Almost every Economist column is named after a person, place or object tied to its subject, so each one is a ready-made identify question. The paper still calls itself a "newspaper", leaves its articles unsigned, and in **2026** Canadian billionaire **Stephen Smith** bought the Rothschild family's **26.9%** stake.

### The basics

| Fact | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Founded | **September 1843** by **James Wilson**, a Scottish hat-maker, to campaign against the **Corn Laws**. His son-in-law **Walter Bagehot** later edited it. |
| Style | Calls itself a newspaper, not a magazine. Articles carry no bylines (the editor writes the leaders). The red logo box. |
| Editor | **Zanny Minton Beddoes** (since 2015), its first woman editor. |
| Owners (2026) | **Exor** (the Agnelli family) about 43%; **Stephen Smith** 26.9% (bought from the Rothschilds in Mar 2026); trustees protect editorial independence; no one may vote more than 20%. |
| Spin-offs | ***1843*** magazine (named after the founding year); **Economist Impact**; **EIU** (Economist Intelligence Unit, which runs the Democracy Index and Liveability Index). |
| Daily products | The **Espresso** app (a short daily briefing); ***The Intelligence*** daily podcast. |
| Annual | ***The World Ahead*** (formerly *The World in 20XX*). |
| Indexes | The **Big Mac index** (1986). The **glass-ceiling index** (best countries for working women). |

### The columns and their namesakes

| Column | Covers | Named after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bagehot** | Britain | **Walter Bagehot**, the third editor, author of *The English Constitution* and *Lombard Street*. |
| **Lexington** | The United States | The **Battle of Lexington** (1775), the first shots of the American Revolution. James Bennet (ex-New York Times) became the first American to write it. |
| **Charlemagne** | Europe | **Charlemagne**, the first ruler to unite much of western Europe. |
| **Banyan** | Asia | The **banyan tree**, under which Asian villages meet. |
| **Chaguan** | China | *Chaguan* means **teahouse**, where Chinese people gossip and talk business. |
| **Bello** | Latin America | **Andrés Bello**, a 19th-century Venezuelan-Chilean scholar. |
| **Schumpeter** | Business | **Joseph Schumpeter**, the economist of "creative destruction". |
| **Buttonwood** | Finance and markets | The **Buttonwood Agreement** (1792), signed under a tree, which founded the NYSE. |
| **Free exchange** | Economics | Free trade, an idea the paper has backed since 1843. |
| **Bartleby** | Work and management | *Bartleby, the Scrivener*, Herman Melville's office clerk who "would prefer not to". |
| **Johnson** | Language | **Samuel Johnson**, the English dictionary-maker. |
| **Babbage** | Science and technology (podcast) | **Charles Babbage**, the computing pioneer. |
| **Baobab** | Africa (an earlier column) | The **baobab tree**. |

Columnists rotate and stay anonymous, so learn the namesakes, not the writers.

## Global papers and magazines

The classic quiz trio: the **FT** is printed on **salmon-pink** paper and owned by Japan's **Nikkei**; the **WSJ** draws portraits as dot "**hedcuts**"; and **Forbes** and **Fortune** are best known for their **lists**. 2026 deals to know: **Axel Springer** bought the UK's *Telegraph* (about $766 m), and **James Murdoch** bought *New York* magazine and other Vox Media titles.

## Indian business media

The connects to know: *Business Standard* is owned by the **Kotak** family. *Mint* launched in partnership with the **WSJ**. **Reliance** (via Network18) owns CNBC-TV18 and Moneycontrol, and **Adani** owns NDTV Profit. *Moneylife* was founded by **Sucheta Dalal and Debashis Basu**, the reporters behind *The Scam*. And **The Ken** has no "Espresso": that is The Economist's app. The Ken's daily story is backed by newsletters such as *The Nutgraf* and *First Principles*.
