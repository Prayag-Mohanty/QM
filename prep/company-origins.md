Two of the most reliable business-quiz seams: what a company sold *before* it sold the thing it is famous for, and who actually founded it as opposed to who gets the credit. Indian brands are at the end.

## Quiz-ready stories

1. **Sony's** first product was an electric **rice cooker** — a wooden bucket with aluminium electrodes, no thermostat. It never reached market and is kept in Sony's own archive as a monument to failure.
2. **Nintendo** was founded in **1889** to make handmade **hanafuda playing cards** in Kyoto. It later printed **Disney characters** on them, decades before Mario.
3. **Wipro** began in **1945** as **Western India Vegetable Products** — hydrogenated cooking fat and vegetable oil.
4. **Toyota** was a division of **Toyoda Automatic Loom Works**; the loom company is still one of Toyota Motor's biggest shareholders.
5. **Wrigley** sold **soap**, gave away **baking powder** free, then gave away **gum** free with the baking powder. Each freebie outsold its host. Two pivots in a year.
6. **McDonald's** was founded by the **McDonald brothers**. Ray Kroc was a milkshake-machine salesman who bought the franchising rights in 1955 and later bought them out.
7. **Starbucks** sold only **beans and equipment** — no espresso drinks — until Howard Schultz, a marketing hire, bought the company in 1987.
8. **Lamborghini** made **tractors** from surplus WWII hardware; the supercar was built out of spite after Enzo Ferrari dismissed his clutch complaint.
9. **3M** was founded to mine **corundum** for sandpaper. The mine was worthless, so it made sandpaper instead, then adhesives, then Post-its.
10. **IBM** began as a merger of firms making **meat slicers, coffee grinders, time clocks and cheese cutters**.
11. **Avon's** founder was a **door-to-door book salesman** who handed out perfume samples to get doors opened. The perfume outsold the books.
12. **Bombardier** was a **snowmobile** maker before it was an aerospace giant.
13. **Berkshire Hathaway** was a **textile mill**; Buffett bought the dying business and used its cash flow to buy insurers.
14. **Samsung** was founded in 1938 as a trading company exporting **dried fish, vegetables and noodles**.
15. **Nike** was **Blue Ribbon Sports** until 1971; Bill Bowerman poured the first waffle sole in his **kitchen waffle iron**.

### John Ternus

Apple's new CEO is worth knowing properly.

- A **mechanical engineer**, University of Pennsylvania 1997, where he also swam competitively. His senior thesis was a **head-controlled mechanical feeding arm** for people with quadriplegia.
- His first job was designing **VR headsets** at Virtual Research Systems, years before Apple's spatial computing push.
- He joined Apple's product design team in July 2001; his first project was the **Apple Cinema Display**.
- VP Hardware Engineering 2013 → iPhone hardware 2020 → SVP Hardware Engineering 2021 → Apple Watch 2022 → design added end-2025. Each expansion was read as a succession signal.
- The announcement came on **20 April 2026**, the week of Apple's **50th anniversary**; he took over on **1 September 2026**.

### Domino's

- Founded **9 December 1960** in Ypsilanti, Michigan: the brothers borrowed $900 and bought a pizzeria called **DomiNick's** for $500.
- Within eight months **James traded his half of the business to Tom for the used Volkswagen Beetle** they delivered with.
- The original owner wouldn't let them use "DomiNick's" across multiple shops. An employee, **Jim Kennedy**, suggested "Domino's" after a delivery run. The logo's **three dots are the three original stores**, with a dot meant to be added per new store — abandoned almost immediately. It still has three dots across 21,000+ stores.
- Tom Monaghan was orphaned at four, raised partly in Catholic orphanages, served in the Marines, later owned the **Detroit Tigers** (1983–92) and founded Ave Maria University.

## Indian brands

| Brand | Story |
| --- | --- |
| **Amaron** | Made by **Amara Raja**. *Amara* is Sanskrit for "**immortal**" — fitting for a zero-maintenance, long-life battery. |
| **V-Guard → Wonderla** | **Kochouseph Chittilappilly** started V-Guard in Thrissur in 1977 with ₹1 lakh and two employees, making voltage stabilisers. In 2000 he opened Kerala's first water theme park, **Veegaland**, later rebranded **Wonderla**. Same founder, unrelated categories. |
| **Nirma** | **Karsanbhai Patel**, a government lab chemist, mixed detergent in his backyard in 1969 and sold it door to door by bicycle at **₹3/kg** against Surf's ₹13. Named after his daughter **Nirupama**, who died in a car accident; her image is still on the pack. |
| **Thums Up** | Created by **Parle** after Coca-Cola left India in 1977 rather than comply with **FERA**. Spiced with cardamom, cinnamon and nutmeg. The "**b**" was dropped from "Thumbs Up" to make it **trademarkable**. Coca-Cola bought it back in **1993** for about $60 million, when it held **85%** of the market — buying the drink that had filled its own vacuum. |
| **MRF** | Began in 1946 as a **toy balloon** maker in a Madras shed under K.M. Mappillai, before tread rubber and tyres. |
| **Godrej** | Ardeshir Godrej trained as a **lawyer**, tried surgical instruments and locks first. His soap was the **first Indian soap made without animal fat**, using vegetable oil — which let it pass both Hindu and Muslim objections during the Swadeshi movement. |
| **Onida** | An acronym: **O**ur **N**ational **I**ndian **D**omestic **A**ppliances. Its devil mascot sold the line "Neighbour's Envy, Owner's Pride" — unusually, a **villain** as a brand mascot. |
| **Voltas** | A 1954 joint venture between Swiss trading house **Volkart Brothers** and **Tata** — the name is a portmanteau of the two. |
